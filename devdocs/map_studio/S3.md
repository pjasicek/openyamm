## What exists

**Room shapes (`core/Polygon`, `core/IndoorLowering`).** A room's `outline` may bend inward and carry points in a
line; it must not cross itself. Floors and ceilings are cut into convex pieces that use only the outline's own
points, so every edge still meets its neighbours corner to corner. A corridor is a `path` with a `width`.
`slope: {to, toward, steps}` makes a ramp (no steeper than the engine walks, about 45 degrees) or a flight of
steps (each at most 40 units; the engine climbs 50): treads, risers, side walls cut at every step, and the last
riser as the wall under the doorway at the top. `pillars` stand floor to ceiling; `platforms` are stepped onto.
`walls` gives single walls their own surface. Objects stand on the floor where they are, also on a slope.

**Doors and lifts.** A door of `kind: hinged` swings away from whoever opens it on a hinge at the left or right of
its doorway. A lift (`objects.yml` `lifts`) is a block of floor an event raises and lowers; to the engine it is a
door whose faces are walked on. Switches and chests have default surfaces, so neither needs a texture name.

[examples/shapes_gallery](../../openyamm-studio/examples/shapes_gallery) has one of each, and its scenario walks
them with the party's height asserted on the dais, half way up the steps, in the gallery, half way down the ramp
and in the cave. That walk found the first version's mistake: pillars and platforms were built as nodes of their
own, which the compiler drops, and the party walked through the dais at floor height. They are part of the room's
node now.

**Wiring without scripts.** Conditions the schema already named are now built: `gold`, `stat` (any selector of
the game's scripts), `award`, `autonote`, `all`. Events carry `effects` and `else_effects`: gold, add, subtract and
set on any selector or map variable, autonotes, quest bits, items, texts, and a line of Lua for the rest. New
Sorpigal's five wells, five scripts in S2, are five text presets now and still equal to the shipped handlers.

**A script reference read from the game** (`oymap script-reference`): the support script is run and what it
defines is listed: 220 helper functions, the variable selectors (Gold, HP, BaseLuck ...) and the enumerations.
With the list of `evt.` functions read from the engine's registration (S2), nothing about the scripts' vocabulary
is kept by hand.

**Projects read directly.** `audit`, `flow`, `solve`, `render`, `metrics` and `probe` take a project folder in
place of a map: the project is built into a private folder and read as the game would read it, with no install.
`oymap new` makes an empty project; `map_new` is the fifteenth MCP tool.

**Calibration reading** (`calibration/compare.py <project> <original metrics>`): every metric of a rebuild beside
the original's, with its ratio and a reading against version 1 bands (in within a quarter, near within a half).
The bands are deliberately wide and are mine, not measured; CALIBRATION.md's bands are still words.

What the summary line does not say fairly, and why the bands need work before anything is called calibrated:

- **Room counts are not comparable.** A flight of stairs is a room of its own in a sketch, so the Temple has 42
  sectors against the original's 30 and reads "near" or "out" on every count and on the smallest-room sizes. The
  comparison should count rooms of the plan, not sectors.
- **"Reached at the start" depends on doors**, and an L3 sketch has none yet (51 against the Fortress's 4 is the
  sketch being open, not wrong). It belongs to the stage that places doors.
- The bands themselves (a quarter, a half) are mine and wide. They were not derived from how much the four
  originals differ from each other, which is what CALIBRATION.md asks for.

## Findings

- **A crash and a permanent lock.** An event whose chest or door had been refused for another reason was looked up
  as if it existed. Such an event is now skipped. The lock holds its writer's process number, and a lock whose
  writer no longer runs is taken over.
- **A hinged leaf that blocked nothing passed its playtest.** The compiler stores a sliding leaf in its open pose;
  written out as a rotating mover unchanged, the leaf hung in the wall above its doorway, and clicking it then
  walking through passed. The studio's own solve disagreed with the game (the closet was reachable without the
  door), which is how it was caught. The check now requires the solve to show the door closing the closet off.
- **Reading a project wrote into it.** The first project preview built in place and left an `ids.lock.yml` in one
  of openyamm-fe's briefs. Previews build a private copy now; the stray file is removed.
- **The compiler keeps only nodes it knows by prefix** (ROOM_, PORTAL_, MECH_, TRIGGER_) or that the metadata
  names. Geometry under any other name disappears without a word. The build's self-check counts rooms, portals and
  doors, not faces; a face count per room would have caught it.
- **The engine's limits are now in the build:** 50 units of step (the build allows 40), a floor normal of at
  least 0.71 for a ramp. Both were read from `game/indoor/IndoorMovementController.cpp`, not assumed; my first
  step limit was a guess that happened to be safe.
- **The 512 grid of the brief sketches was never the build's rule.** Walls are as thick as the gap between two
  rooms' facing walls (0 to 512). The first sketches were drawn on the grid and overstated floor area for that
  reason alone.
- **An agent's cost follows the vocabulary, not the plumbing.** The same task fell from about 75 tool calls to 31
  when presets could say what the handlers do.
