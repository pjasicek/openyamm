# Map Studio S5-0: the spikes (2026-11-14)

Phase S5-0 of [S5_BROWSER_EDITOR.md](S5_BROWSER_EDITOR.md) section 6.4: seven spikes that measure before S5a builds
anything. The user gave the go at 15:11; all seven reported by 16:58. Reports (saved by the lead from each agent's
final message): `output/s5_spikes/1_viewer/REPORT.md` (spikes 1 and 5), `2_latency/`, `3_walk/`, `4_rpc/`,
`6_wasm22/`, `7_forms/`, each with its scripts and raw data beside it. Spike code is thrown away; the one thing kept
is the request list in `openyamm-studio/schema/rpc/` (spike 4). The design changes the spikes call for are in the
design document as Revision 4 (its section 0.6). The spikes changed nothing in `engine/`, `game/` or `editor/` (the
game's `pin <text>` command, made the same afternoon, is S5a1's work done early); the core fixes they led to are
listed under "Core bugs found".

**Verdict:** S5-0's exit criteria are met (section "S5-0 exit"). Two things stay open and do not block S5a1: the
user's hand check of `F5` and `Ctrl K`, and a re-measure of the indoor 300 ms on a quiet machine.

**The machine and its load.** 32 threads, RTX 3060 Ti (Vulkan 1.4.329), Wayland, Node 24.21.0, GCC 15.2.0 with glibc
2.43, Emscripten 4.0.10. The spikes ran side by side with other sessions; the load average was 4 to 35 through the
afternoon, so every number below carries the load it was taken at. Medians are robust to it; maxima and 95th
percentiles are not.

route) and a three.js 0.186.1 viewer on WebGL2, drawing Castle Darkmoor's rebuild (4 234 faces, 9 979 triangles, 48
textures, 410 markers) and Harmondale's (4 427 building faces, 32 258 terrain triangles, 117 textures, 6 land layers,

Geometry came from `oymap export` JSON, standing in for the missing `view.mesh`. 1600 x 900, antialiased, the whole

## Spike 5: the browsers

**BC textures** (load 6.3; `assets_dev` holds BC7 10 257, BC5 2 584, BC1 2 402 files, no BC4). Every browser tested
exposes s3tc, s3tc_srgb, bptc and rgtc in WebGL2: Chrome 153 on NVIDIA, Chrome 153 headless (SwiftShader), with
`--use-angle=swiftshader`, on Mesa lavapipe, Playwright's Chromium 156 headless shell (the CI default), Firefox 157 on
NVIDIA and on llvmpipe. Decoded against NVIDIA: BC7 identical everywhere, BC5 within 3 levels, BC1 within 1. WebGPU BC
only on the real GPU and lavapipe; Firefox has no `navigator.gpu`. **Verdict:** no CPU BC decode is needed; 4.11's
fallback is dropped; CI's screenshot tests allow a few levels on BC1 and BC5.

**Local network access** (load 12.2; a page on https 127.0.0.1 marked public):

| Browser | Without permission | With permission |
|---|---|---|
| Chrome 153 | fetch fails in 5 ms, WebSocket errors; the permission becomes "denied" | both work |
| Chromium 156 | fetch fails, WebSocket errors | both work |
| Firefox 157 | fetch and WebSocket hang (8 s) waiting for the prompt | both work |
| A page not marked public (Chrome) | both work | |

**Verdict:** pairing (2.12) works after a prompt that gates fetch **and** the WebSocket, in Firefox as well as
Chromium. Design change: pairing starts from a button that says the browser will ask; a first request that hangs
reads as "waiting for your answer" (Firefox), one that fails at once as refused (Chrome). Local mode needs no
permission. R10 confirmed.

**`F5` and `Ctrl K`.** The page receives both first and can `preventDefault` them (Chrome 153, Firefox 157, headless
and headed). Automation cannot show whether the browser then skips its own action. Firefox's shipped key table
(`browser.xhtml`) does not reserve `F5` or `Ctrl K` (it reserves `Ctrl W`); Chrome is unconfirmed. **Open:** the
user's hand check (FOR_THE_USER.md item 10). Until it is done, 3.17's fallback keys (`Ctrl Enter`, `Ctrl P`) stay
named.

commit: the commit plus 200 ms. Brush: one commit per stroke (1.3 s today, about 0.5 s with kept land), and a keyed or
path patch for `heights.nodes`.

## Spike 3: walk

**Measured.** The game's `IndoorMovementController` and `OutdoorMovementController`, game sources compiled
unchanged with the studio's Emscripten 4.0.10, in a browser worker in headless Chrome 153 and Firefox 159, on built
bytes: Darkmoor's rebuild (4 659 faces, 268 sectors), Harmondale's (4 859 faces, 109 models, 294 colliders) and MM9's
Thjorgard city as the stress case (30 605 faces). A 180 s recorded walk per map: 10 800 frames, 23 040 controller
steps (1/128 s), 29 teleports. The worker needs the map bytes, the scene text, `decoration_data.txt` (31 KB) and,
outdoors, a 32 KB terrain pack; no PhysicsFS.

| What | Result | Load |
|---|---|---|
| Walk step (one 1/60 s frame, 2-3 controller steps) | median 10-40 us, p99 55-260 us; single frames up to 3.8 ms (Firefox, Harmondale), 1.2-1.7 ms for the first frame after load | 12.6-17.8 |
| Paced rate | 60.05 steps a second on every map, both browsers | 12.6-17.8 |
| Run 1 at higher load | same medians, p99 55-435 us, single frames to 8.5 ms (load, GC or tier-up; natively 45 us) | 25-33 |
| Native and Node wasm | Darkmoor 12.5 / 12.9 us mean; Thjorgard 36.1 / 38.3; wasm 1-1.5x native in Node, 1.5-2.5x in browsers | 15.2 |
| `walk.toggle` worst case (full cache rebuild) | Darkmoor 0.66-0.8 ms, Thjorgard 3.7-4.4 ms (to 6.8) | 12.6-17.8 |
| `walk.load` | 20-70 ms indoors; Harmondale 194-290 ms, of which yaml-cpp on the 960 KB scene 190-280 (the ODM parses in 2-6) | 12.6-17.8 |
| Worker memory; size | 20-35 MB; the walk's wasm 1 032 668 bytes, 360 830 gzip, 262 446 brotli | |

**Parity.** With C-library `sinf`/`cosf` (glibc against Emscripten's musl), Harmondale's walk differed on 79 lines and
Thjorgard's on 45 (1 ulp to 1e-3 units) and converged; Darkmoor was identical. With deterministic `sin`/`cos` of the
yaw in the walk driver, all three are bit-identical natively and in wasm, and Node, Chrome and Firefox gave the same
bytes (12 browser runs). The controllers themselves showed no difference. Probes: `sinf`/`cosf` differ on 2.5 % of the
recorded yaws; over 200 000 random inputs `tanh` 13 %, `sin`/`cos` 1.3 %, `bx::sqrt` 84 %, `bx::length` 28 %,
`std::sqrt` 0 % (bx has no SIMD in this Emscripten build). Not hit but able to differ: the rotations of
`IndoorMovers.cpp` and `OutdoorGeometryUtils`, two `bx::length` in `IndoorGeometryUtils`. Not covered: doors toggled by
hand (worst case measured instead), ladders, water, swimming, actors, outdoor bounds, MM9's own decoration rows,
event-hidden decorations.

**Verdict.** D7 holds; the 1 ms step budget and 60 steps a second are **met** on 30 605 faces; the native-lane
fallback is not needed. Design changes: the walk's input must not depend on C-library trigonometry (the core's own
`sin`/`cos`, shared with spike 6's math, also for the game's mover rotations); the contract suite's recorded walk
includes a map with a rotating mover; `walk.load` takes the scene's movement state instead of the whole scene text.

## Spike 4: the request list

**Kept** in `openyamm-studio/schema/rpc/`: `common.rpc.json` (`x-rpc-version: 1`, error codes, revision, file names
and trees, an issue with severity, 11 typed error kinds), `session` (`hello`; `configure`, host and worker only),
`project` (`open`, `read`, `status`), `edit` (`apply` with `add`, `set`, `remove`, `patch`), `check` (`validate`,
`things`), `build` (`run`), `pins` (`list`); `gen.mjs` (catalogue lint and TypeScript generator, plain Node, `--check`
fails on a stale file); `generated/rpc.ts`; the contract suite (`contract/`, 40 fixture calls on three example
projects, `--project` for read-only calls on real ones). The dispatcher and handlers (`cli/Rpc.*`, 882 lines), the
wasm entry (`wasm/RpcModule.cpp`, `oymap_call`) and the embed script live in the spike's copy, to be moved into the
core in S5a2.

**Choices.** A validation layer (RPC schemas embedded like the project schemas, params checked by
`SchemaSet::validateAt` before the handler, results checked by the suite with ajv), not generated C++ structs.
JSON-RPC codes with `data.kind` per error; a refusal is a typed error, not `ok: false` (except `check.validate`).
The asset root belongs to the session, never a param; no output paths. Names `<group>.<name>` (`edit.apply`,
`build.run`, `check.things`); the revision opaque (12 to 64 hex digits). Version 1 promises only what the core does
today; `author`, `label`, `merge_key`, `rebase`, core ids, `rename`, `replace_file`, `set_value`, the pins counter and
`x-key` come later as additions. `patch` is RFC 6902 with the keyed-pointer departure of 2.6 (keyed by `id` until
`x-key`); an object's `/id` and `ids.lock.yml` cannot be patched. `edit.apply` returns the canonical tree of every
changed file. Closed objects; every non-integer number states `x-decimals`; world positions integers; no absolute
paths in results.

| What (load 13.7 / 11.7) | Native serve | Wasm `oymap_call` in Node |
|---|---|---|
| 40 fixture calls valid against the schemas | 40/40 | 40/40 |
| Expected outcome | 40/40 | 36/40 |
| Byte-identical to native | | 36/40 |
| Read-only calls on Darkmoor, Erathia, Harmondale: valid / identical | 21/21 | 21/21 / 16/21 |

Every wasm difference was the missing asset root (spike 6). Timings native/wasm in ms (load 12-14): `project.open`
Darkmoor 142/171, Erathia 174/162, Harmondale 126/148; `check.validate` with assets 402/577, 1 690/1 598,
1 554/1 378; `build.run` dry 888/1 041, 1 647/1 336, 983/817; `check.things` 899, 1 874, 1 327 (native only);
`edit.apply` on the slice dungeon 28-50 / 62-172. The first call parses the embedded schemas (41-73 ms); a params
refusal then costs 0.1-0.2 ms. `project.read` and `pins.list` cost 35-90 ms because each call reloads the project for
its revision; the cache removes that. TypeScript 5.6.3 `--strict` compiles a client in 0.9 s and rejects unknown
methods and missing params. The wasm module is 7.4 MB raw with names, 2.2 MB gzip.

**Verdict.** D2 holds. Design changes: ids unique per file, so operations, pins, selections and provenance carry the
file; the severities' first rule exists; the contract suite keeps one wasm instance alive across calls; later an
`x-discriminator: op` hint on the operation union and contract cases for `locked` and `failed`. A mistake: `npm
install` without a `package.json` wrote into the user's home folder (FOR_THE_USER.md item 11).

## Spike 6: native against wasm on all 22 projects

**Measured.** A snapshot at 15:13 (HEAD a761ce68bd plus 137 changed files); `oymap build <copy> --assets <root>` on a
fresh copy of each of the 22 projects (15 indoor, 7 outdoor), natively (GCC 15.2.0, libstdc++, glibc 2.43) and in
Node wasm (Emscripten 4.0.10: clang, libc++, musl), both `-O2 -ffp-contract=off`, every output file compared.

| Run | Identical | Differences |
|---|---|---|
| Wasm as the studio has it | 15 (all indoor) | 7 outdoor `build.json` (the asset root missing); Erathia's `.odm` 13 bytes |
| Wasm reading the asset root | 21 | Erathia's `.odm` 13 bytes |
| Both with the UB fixes | 22 | none; the fixed native build equals the unfixed one on all 22 |

**The asset root in Node wasm**: `PHYSFS_init` needs a user directory; Emscripten's `HOME=/home/web_user` does not
exist under `NODERAWFS`. Fixed in the copy by a ten-line `--pre-js` passing `HOME`, `XDG_DATA_HOME` and `OYMAP_*` from
Node, and by removing an `#ifdef __EMSCRIPTEN__ assetsReadable = false` in `core/OutdoorBuild.cpp`. The browser
worker's PhysicsFS will need a HOME in its file system too (S5c).

**Erathia's 13 bytes** are not trigonometry: one post of `thing:docks` (a deck, posts 64 on an edge 61.44 long) gets
`std::clamp(v, lo, hi)` with `lo` 32 > `hi` 29.44, undefined behaviour that libstdc++ answers with `hi` and libc++ with
`lo`.

**libm**: glibc and musl differ on 5.4 % of 3.2 million distinct `hypot` inputs and on 1-3 % of `sin`, `cos`, `tan`,
`atan2`, `cbrt` (1 ulp, three cases of 2); neither is correctly rounded; no difference reaches a byte (the lowering
rounds to whole units). musl's math sources compiled natively reproduce wasm bit for bit on all 3.2 million inputs,
leave the 22 native builds byte-identical to glibc's, and cost nothing (best of 3, load about 4: Darkmoor 0.98 s glibc,
0.84 musl, 1.48 wasm; Erathia 1.66, 1.69, 2.33).

**Sanitizers** (native ASan/UBSan with `_GLIBCXX_ASSERTIONS`, wasm ASan) found five UB sites, two natively and three
more in wasm (table under "Core bugs found"); one is hit by 17 of the 22 builds; all give the same bytes today; one
crashed an instrumented wasm build. After the fixes both sanitizers are clean on all 22 and the outputs are equal.

**Time and memory** (load 20-25): indoor 0.28-1.25 s and 35-69 MB natively, 0.66-1.56 s and 346-407 MB in Node wasm;
outdoor 0.55-3.45 s and 53-125 MB, 1.14-2.49 s and 185-244 MB; all 22 about 18 s natively, 25 s in wasm. Wasm indoor
uses about 300 MB more than native (the heap never shrinks), not investigated. `oymap.wasm` 7.94 MB. Under wasm ASan a
build takes 1.5 to 26 minutes.

**Verdict.** 4.10's S5-0 requirement is **met**: the Node wasm build reads an asset root, and the one difference is
located and fixed in the copy. Design change to 4.10's emphasis: the risk to byte identity is undefined behaviour
answered differently by the two C++ standard libraries, not libm. So: a sanitizer job over the 22 projects (native
ASan/UBSan with `_GLIBCXX_ASSERTIONS`, and wasm ASan, each finding sites the other missed); the 22-project comparison
in CI (about 45 s for both sides at load 20); musl's math compiled into the core on both sides as insurance (it also
removes the dependence on Emscripten's libc version); no `long double` in lowering code (or its products under 2^64
by construction). PLAN's rule that a map's bytes decide whether saves keep their map state holds for these 22 maps.
