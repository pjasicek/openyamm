# Indoor rotating movers and ladders (2026-10-30)

Status: **implemented** (phase 2, 2026-10-30): the sections below describe the code; "Implementation notes" lists
where it differs from the first design and every renderer change. Scope: hinged doors, levers and switches, pivoting floor and wall traps and other rotating brushes, and
climbable ladders in native indoor maps (BLV), opt-in through map data, first used by the MM9 rework's
[Anskram Keep](../level_generation/projects/mm9r_anskramkeep/README.md) pivot traps. MM6–MM8 maps carry none of the new
data and must behave and render exactly as before.

## Inventory: what MM9 rotates (all 45 DAT worlds)

Read with the shared DAT reader (`read_dat_world`), every object of every world; LithTech (LT) units, Y up, native =
LT × 2.56. **1 051 rotating objects in 37 of the 45 worlds**, plus **68 ladder volumes** in 21 worlds.

**Classes.** `RotatingDoor` (1 017), `Switch` (19, the same keys) and `RotatingBrush` (15, continuous spinners:
`X/Y/ZAxisRevTime`, `SpinUpTime`, `SpinDownTime`). `Door`, `Lift`, `WeightedLift` and `Button` are always linear
(`MoveDir`, `MoveDist`), including the 68 whose names suggest rotation (`PivotButton`, `PivotTreasurePanel`,
`LeverPanel`, the Dungeon of Secrets' fake levers, the Arena's four "RotatingDoor"s, which slide 76). The dump is
regenerated by `level_generation/projects/mm9r_common/rotating_inventory.py` (`rotating_inventory.json`).

**Encoding.** `RotationPoint` is the world-space pivot; `RotationAngles` = degrees about LT X, Y, Z from the authored
(closed) pose to the open pose, exactly one component non-zero on all but 7 objects, in world axes (only the 4 Chasm of
the Dead pendulums have an object yaw). `Speed`/`ClosingSpeed` in degrees per second (`ClosingSpeed` 0: never returns).
Behaviour: `CloseWaitTime` (auto-close delay), `MoveDelay`, `OpenWaitTime`, `Sticky` (toggles, stays), `PushOpen`
(opens on use), `OpenAway` (swings away from the user), `ReopenOnContact` (a blocked door reverses),
`AITriggerable` (monsters open it), `Locked`, `StartOpen`, `TouchToOpen`, `Waveform` (0–3, most likely linear, sine,
slow-off, slow-on), sounds (`Open/CloseStart/Busy/StopSound`, `SoundRadius`), trigger wiring
(`Open/CloseTriggerTarget0-3` with `toggle`, `lock`, `unlock`, ...), `PortalName`, `DoubleDoorName`, and `AttachTo`
(36 parent/child movers: saws on trap arms, gears on a lift, bookcases on hinge helpers).

## What the engine has today

- **Indoor movers are linear only** (`MapDeltaDoor`, the BLV/DLV door record: `direction` in 16.16, `move_length`,
  `open_speed`/`close_speed` in units per second, `x/y/z_offsets` = each vertex in the open pose; runtime distance 0 =
  open, `move_length` = closed). MM6–MM8 have nothing else: OpenEnroth's door attributes are `DOOR_TRIGGERED`,
  `DOOR_SETTING_UP` and `DOOR_NOSOUND`, and no BLV in the three games has a rotating door or a ladder (MM's "turning"
  wheels and fans are animated textures). There is no partial indoor rotation support to build on.
- **The vertex pose of a door** is computed in two places: `applyIndoorMechanismDoorToVertices`
  (`IndoorGeometryUtils.cpp`, used by `buildIndoorMechanismAdjustedVertices`, the world runtime's collision cache,
  the movement controller's cache and the bake scene export) and a private copy in
  `IndoorRenderer::updateMechanismRenderVerticesForDoorIds`. All vertex arrays are integer (`IndoorVertex`).
- **State and time**: `EventRuntime::advanceMechanisms` (120 Hz steps from `IndoorSceneRuntime::advanceMechanisms`)
  and `EventRuntime::applyMechanismAction` (open, close, trigger; a reversal mid-move keeps the distance) drive
  `RuntimeMechanismState` (`state`, `timeSinceTriggeredMs`, `currentDistance`, `rotationDirection`, `isMoving`).
  All of it is saved; `rotationDirection` exists for the outdoor rotating doors and is unused indoors.
- **Collision** reads the moved vertices: the changed doors' faces are invalidated in the face geometry caches
  (`refreshMechanismRuntimeGeometryCache`, `IndoorMovementController::applyMechanismGeometryUpdate`). Candidate faces
  come from the sector lists `floorFaceIds`/`wallFaceIds`/`ceilingFaceIds` of the body's rooms and the rooms behind
  the portals it touches; floor sampling reads `floorFaceIds` only. A face's kind comes from its static `facetType`
  (the compiler writes 1, 3, 4, 5 or 6 from the compiled normal) or, for type 0, from its current normal.
- **Moving bodies**: vertical doors carry what rests on them (`applyMovingMechanismSupportMovement` for the party each
  mechanism step, `carryRestingObjectsOnMovedFloors` for actors, corpses and items). Nothing pushes or blocks: a
  door moving into the party passes through it.
- **Portals** are static faces in the converted MM9 maps (doorway portals at the leaves' face; the leaves lie wholly
  in one room). MM6–MM8 door portals are door-moved faces that collapse when shut.
- **Baked lighting** of door faces ([indoor baked lighting](INDOOR_BAKED_LIGHTING.md), "Doors and other linear
  movers"): a face one door moves rigidly, travelling at most 1024, is baked in the BLV's stored pose and samples its
  lightmap while its current vertices are one translation of the stored ones (`IndoorBakedLighting::faceLightmap`,
  UVs at `p - t`). The lightmap UV stream is built per batch and stays valid while the face moves rigidly.
- **Outdoors** (the old MM9 bmodel_world imports) already rotate BModels: `OutdoorBModelMechanism` with
  `motion.rotation.pivot_openyamm` and `rotation_angles_openyamm_deg` (Euler X then Y then Z, linear in time),
  `open_away` (sign chosen from the activator), `move_party` (translation carry only). That is prior art for the
  data names and the open-away rule, not shared code: BModels are rebuilt per frame there.
- **No ladders** anywhere (no climb state indoors or out). The party's indoor movement has a flying branch
  (`resolveMove(..., flyingActive)`: no gravity, vertical velocity as given), used for flying actors.

## Data format (opt-in, per mover)

A door record in the scene (`initial_state.doors`) may carry a `mover` block; a door without one is today's linear
door, unchanged. A map with no `mover` block anywhere is loaded, simulated, saved and drawn exactly as today. The block
makes a door rotate (`rotation`) and adds MM9's behaviours; `bodies`, `carry` and `auto_close_seconds` also work on
a linear door that opts in:

```yaml
initial_state:
  doors:
    - door_id: 15
      direction: [0, 0, 0]          # a rotating mover has no linear direction
      move_length: 900              # the swing in tenths of a degree (90.0 degrees)
      open_speed: 700               # tenths of a degree per second (MM9 Speed 70)
      close_speed: 700
      state: 2                      # closed, as for any door
      x_offsets: [...]              # each vertex in the stored (closed) pose
      ...
      mover:
        rotation:                   # absent: the door is linear
          pivot: [-123.0, 4210.0, -491.0]   # native units, a point on the hinge line
          axis: [0.0, 0.0, 1.0]             # unit vector; positive angles turn right-handed about it
          open_degrees: 90.0                # signed angle from the closed pose to the open pose
          open_away: true                   # MM9 OpenAway: the sign is chosen away from the opener
        easing: sine                # linear | sine, rotating movers only (MM9 Waveform 0 | any other)
        bodies: reverse             # ignore | hold | reverse | push: a body in the way (below)
        carry: false                # grounded bodies standing on its faces move with it
        auto_close_seconds: 7       # MM9 CloseWaitTime: an opened door closes itself after this long
```

- **Units.** `move_length`, `open_speed` and `close_speed` keep their meaning as travel and travel per second, the
  travel being tenths of a degree, so the door state machine, `SetDoorState`, reversal mid-move, timers and saves
  need no change. The loader refuses a record whose `move_length` is not `round(|open_degrees| * 10)`, whose
  direction is not zero, whose axis is not unit length, or whose `open_degrees` is outside (0, 360] (the hazards'
  multi-turn swings are later work; the spinners are `spin`, "Continuous spinners").
- **Defaults** are today's behaviour: `easing: linear`, `bodies: ignore`, `carry: false` (a vertical linear door keeps
  its existing carry), no auto-close.
- **`carry` on a linear door that slides level** (direction without a vertical part: a sliding bridge, a tram) makes
  it carry the party, actors and items standing on it along its travel, like a lift (`indoorDoorCarriesPartySupport`,
  since 2026-11-12). Without it a level slide moves out from under what stands on it, as in MM6-MM8. Map Studio
  writes it for its `movers` that slide level and do not stretch.
- **The stored pose is the closed pose.** `x/y/z_offsets` (and the BLV vertex array) hold every vertex closed; with
  `direction` zero the linear formula gives the closed pose at every distance, so a code path that missed the rotation
  would draw the door shut rather than somewhere wrong.
- **Rigidity.** The loader refuses a rotating mover that moves part of a face (every vertex of each of its faces must
  be its own), shares a vertex with another door, or moves a portal face. Rotating faces are written with facet type
  0 (kind from the current normal) and listed in the floor, wall and ceiling lists of each room they are in (the
  compiler does this; a pivoting floor becomes a wall and a wall a floor).
- **Rooms.** A mover's faces belong to the room its closed pose stands in (`roomNumber`). A mover whose swing
  crosses a portal (an `open_away` door, a leaf swinging through its doorway) also names the other room as
  `roomBehindNumber` and is listed in that room's face lists; the renderer, collision and picking already accept a
  face in either of its two rooms. The converter computes the rooms from poses sampled along the swing.
- **Runtime form.** The loader collects the blocks into `IndoorMapData::moverDefinitions` (`IndoorMoverDefinition`,
  sorted by door id, empty on every map without them), map data like `translucentFaces`: never saved, so a save
  cannot carry a stale pivot. Lookups are by door id (`findIndoorMoverDefinition`), null for every MM6–MM8 door.

## Kinematics

- Open fraction `f = 1 - distance / move_length` (0 closed, 1 open), eased `e = ease(f)` (`linear`: `f`; `sine`:
  `(1 - cos(π f)) / 2`), angle `a = s * open_degrees * e`, with `s = ±1` from `rotationDirection` (always +1 unless
  `open_away`). Each vertex: `p = pivot + R(axis, a) (stored - pivot)`, rounded to integers like every vertex array.
  Easing applies to the pose only; time stays linear in distance, and both easings are symmetric
  (`e(f) = 1 - e(1 - f)`), so opening, closing and a reversal mid-swing are continuous. MM9's slow-on/slow-off waveforms
  (2, 3: the three Anskram hatches use 3) would need per-direction easing that jumps at a reversal; they become
  `sine` (user's choice, 2026-10-30).
- **Auto-close** (`auto_close_seconds`): a door that reaches open starts closing after that many seconds
  (`IndoorSceneRuntime::advanceMoverAutoClose`, the time kept in the door's existing `timeSinceTriggeredMs`, so it is
  saved); a click or `SetDoorState` in the meantime behaves as today.
- **One function** computes a door's pose for both kinds: `applyIndoorMechanismDoorToVertices(door, pMover,
  distance, rotationDirection, vertices)` (mover null: today's linear formula, unchanged), used by every caller,
  including the renderer, whose private copy is removed. The pure logic is `game/indoor/IndoorMovers.{h,cpp}`. The rigid transform of the current pose
  (`indoorRotatingMoverMotion`) is what carrying and the lightmap use.
- **Rounding.** Integer vertices put a rotated quad up to about 0.9 units off its plane mid-swing; the Newell normal,
  floor heights and collision tolerate that, and the closed and open poses of axis-aligned 90° swings about an
  integer pivot are exact. The texture UVs are the face's stored per-vertex texels (a rotating face always keeps its
  stored texels: `indoorMoverFaceKeepsStoredTexels` is not asked), so the texture turns with the face.
- **Open away.** When a closed `open_away` mover starts to open, `IndoorSceneRuntime` sets `rotationDirection` to the
  sign that moves the leaf's centre away from the party (`indoorMoverOpenAwayDirection`). It keeps that sign until the
  mover is closed again; it is saved already.
- **Events and sounds** are those of any door: click and `SetDoorState`, `MechanismMoving`/`MechanismStop` sounds as
  today (the per-map mechanism sound, or MM9's per-mover sounds later).

## Collision

### Bodies in the swing (`bodies`)

Run per mechanism step (120 Hz, `IndoorSceneRuntime::applyMoverBodyRules`) for the movers that moved, against the
party's body and the live actors' bodies (vertical capsules against the face polygons, `indoorBodyAxisPolygonDistance`).
A face counts only while it moves toward the body, so a leaf swinging away from a body leaning on it, or a floor
tipping away from under one, stops nothing:

- `hold` (levers): a step that would put a face into a body is withheld (the mechanism's `timeSinceTriggeredMs` and
  distance go back to the previous step; it stays opening or closing) and retried next step.
- `reverse` (MM9 `ReopenOnContact`: the doors and hatches): a closing door that meets a body opens again (and its
  auto-close starts over); an opening door holds. A door never passes through the party or a monster.
- `push` (pivot traps): a face moving into a body moves the body clear of it, away along the shortest way out, and
  the body is no longer supported (`indoorBodyPolygonClearance`, `IndoorPartyRuntime::pushPartyPosition`,
  `IndoorWorldRuntime::displaceActorByMover`). The move is not swept against the world, so `push` suits movers with
  open space on their swept side (a trap's pit); a door or lever uses `reverse` or `hold`.
- `ignore`: today's behaviour (every MM6–MM8 door; decorative or out-of-reach movers).

A step is at most 0.75° at 90°/s (a 250-unit leaf's tip moves about 3 units per step), so the end-pose test of each
step stands for its sweep; a mover faster than 4° per step is tested in sub-steps.

### Standing on a rotating face (`carry`)

The party, grounded on a face of a `carry` mover, is moved by the step's rigid motion of its foot point
(`IndoorSceneRuntime::carryPartyOnRotatingMovers`); its facing does not turn, and actors, corpses and items are not
carried by a rotation (no rotating platform needs that yet). Once the face is steeper than walkable (normal z below
0.687) the party is no longer supported: it slides or falls under the ordinary rules. The movement controller's
mechanism support faces (doors with a vertical direction, `indoorDoorCarriesPartySupport`) include every face of a
rotating `carry` mover, so a former wall arm that has become a floor carries the party; for actors and items such a
face is a floor only if its stored pose was (the sector floor lists are static).

This is how a pivot trap **tips the party** (`bodies: push`, `carry: true`): the half the party stands on turns
down and carries it to the walkable limit, while the upper wall arm sweeps down over that half and pushes the party
along its arc; the party slides and falls into the pit, an ordinary room below the trap's opening, entered through a
horizontal portal at the closed floor's plane. The cross is symmetric, so after 90° the former wall arm is the floor
above the pit and the former floor half the wall. A body on the far half is lifted by it and pushed off. Fall damage
applies as for any fall (635 native in Anskram); the spike pit is the pit's own damage event (below).

## Portals and visibility

No change to `IndoorPortalVisibility`. Rotating movers never move portal faces (refused at load). Doorway portals stay
static faces at the leaves, as for the converted linear doors: an open or closed doorway is always traversed and the
leaves occlude by being drawn. A trap's pit is a room behind a horizontal portal at the trap's opening; while the trap
is shut the pit is behind its faces and costs only its few faces when the portal is in view. A later optimisation, not
part of this work: a portal flagged `closed_by_door` that visibility skips while its door is closed (MM6–MM8 get this
by collapsing door portals).

## Baked lighting on rotating faces

Extend the moving-lightmap rule from "one translation of the stored pose" to "a rigid motion of the stored pose":

- **Bakeable** (`indoorMoverFaceBakeable`): unchanged for linear doors; a rotating mover's faces are bakeable when one
  door moves every vertex (always true, enforced at load) and the farthest any vertex travels along the swing
  (`2 r sin(|a|/2)`, `r` its distance from the axis) is at most `IndoorBakedMoverMaxTravel` (1024). They are baked in
  the stored (closed) pose, as linear movers are.
- **Runtime** (`IndoorBakedLighting::faceLightmap`): a linear door keeps today's check (the vertices must be one
  translation of the stored pose, so MM6–MM8 and every existing bake are unchanged). For a rotating mover the transform
  is known from its definition, not inferred from rounded vertices: `stored = pivot + R⁻¹ (p - pivot)`
  (`IndoorBakedFaceLightmap::currentToStored`), and `IndoorBakedFaceLightmap::uvAt` samples the page there when the
  batch's lightmap UVs are built. The UV stream stays static while the face moves, as for a linear door.
- **What it looks like.** A swung door keeps its closed-pose light (accepted for linear doors and panels already); a
  tipped trap floor carries its corridor light into the pit while it hangs there.
- **Bake scene export**: the rotating movers' stored points as today (the stored pose is all the bake needs). Glass
  on a rotating mover (`<node>_GLASS<k>` parts, each its own door with the same rotation) works as on a linear one:
  the translucent batch's vertices and its one-sided plane normal follow the pose.

## Picking and LOS

Both read the same moved vertex arrays (picking the renderer's, LOS the runtime cache), so they follow the swing once
every pose comes from the one function. Headless picking follows door motion only while frames render (unchanged
limitation, see the Anskram README).

## Saving and loading

No save format change. A rotating mover's state, time and open-away sign are the door's existing saved fields
(`MapDeltaDoor.state/timeSinceTriggered`, `RuntimeMechanismState` with `rotationDirection`); the pivot, axis, angle,
easing and rules are map data rebuilt from the scene on every load. The converted MM9 maps are unreleased, so a save of
an older build of such a map (doors then linear) is not migrated: its door records are those of the older geometry.

## Ladders

### Data

A scene-level `ladders` list (map data, `IndoorMapData::ladders`, empty on every map without one):

```yaml
ladders:
  - id: PitLadder1
    bottom: [1024.0, 6200.0, -890.0]   # foot of the climbable strip on the lower floor (centre of its width)
    top: [1024.0, 6200.0, -491.0]      # where the strip ends (usually the upper floor's height)
    facing_degrees: 90                 # the direction a climber faces (toward the ladder)
    width: 96                          # the strip's width
    reach: 48                          # how far in front of the strip a body may stand and grab it
    top_exit: [1024.0, 6260.0, -491.0] # where the party stands after climbing off at the top
    grab_in_air: false                 # optional: a body in the air grabs it too (MM9's climbing poles)
    grab_at_top: false                 # optional: a body walking from top_exit toward the top takes it there
```

### Entering, climbing, leaving

- **Grab**: a grounded body in front of the strip (from the strip back to `reach` plus its radius, within the strip's
  width and height) moving toward it within 60° of `facing_degrees` attaches. Falling past a ladder does not grab it
  (unless it has `grab_in_air`), and only a ladder with `grab_at_top` is entered from its top (below).
- **Climb**: while attached, gravity is off and movement uses the flying branch of `resolveMove` with vertical
  velocity from input: forward or Fly Up climbs, back or Fly Down descends (192 per second, running 288), strafe moves
  along the strip; the body is held just in front of it. Collision stays on: a shut hatch or ceiling stops the climb.
- **Leave**: climbing past the strip's top glides the party onto `top_exit` (below); descending onto the floor detaches it;
  jump lets go; strafing past the strip's end lets go. Letting go is a fall from that height, no more.
- **Taking it at the top** (`grab_at_top: true`, opt-in; MM9's converters set it by default, below): a standing body at
  `top_exit`'s height (±48), within `reach` plus its radius of the strip's top edge (the hole's edge, not anywhere
  around `top_exit`, which can lie 150 back), walking within 60° toward its hold spot, glides onto the strip 16 under
  its top, held in front of it across from where it stood (a radius inside the strip's ends; refused when that spot is
  outside the map or inside a face, and when a floor lies over it within 32 of the party's feet: the way down is a
  hole, and a shut trap's floor over a pit's ladder, Anskram's pivot traps and the Chasm's, covers it). On such a ladder, movement that is neither
  toward nor away from the strip climbs when it points toward `top_exit` and descends when it points away, so walking on
  from the exit carries the party down and walking back brings it up; strafing then does not let go (jump does).
- **The glide at the top** (2026-11-09): getting on at the top and climbing off it both move the party there over a
  short eased glide (about walking speed, at least 0.2 s), across at the higher end's height and then down, or up and
  then across, so it never cuts the floor's edge. Until 2026-11-09 both were a jump of up to about 200 units (Dark
  Passageway's Ladder5, whose `top_exit` is 154 behind the strip). Input is ignored during the glide, which counts as
  climbing (`IndoorPartyRuntime::climbingLadder`); a teleport ends it.
- **Floor portals**: a climb (or a fall) passes a horizontal portal like any other. Over the hole the upper room has no
  floor face and the lower room's bounds end at the portal, so the eye's sector lookup (`findIndoorSectorForPoint`) finds
  nothing and refuses the move. Native BLVs mark such a room with sector flag `0x8`, which makes its floor portals count
  in that lookup; the compiler now sets it on the front room of every up-facing portal (a BLV compiled before 2026-10-30
  needs a rebuild). Anskram Keep never showed this: its crosses and hatches stretch the pit rooms' bounds upward.
- **State**: `IndoorPartyRuntime::m_ladderIndex` (runtime only: a save made while climbing restores the party falling
  from where it was).
- **Controls**: desktop uses the existing movement keys plus Fly Up and Fly Down (already bound, outdoor-only today);
  Android shows its flight arrows also while the party is on a ladder (`touch_controls.yml` note and the touch input's
  visibility rule), and the movement stick works as forward and back. Turn-based mode moves on a ladder like walking.
- **Monsters** do not use ladders (MM9's monsters do not either); the indoor pathfinder adds no link for a ladder, and
  flyers fly. A ladder is not an obstacle (it is a volume, not faces); a converter keeps MM9's `LadderBlocker` brushes
  as invisible collision where they matter.

### Leaning ladders (2026-10-31)

A ladder whose `top` is not straight above its `bottom` (more than 1 unit apart horizontally) leans. It is climbed along
the line from bottom to top: every test that an upright ladder makes against its bottom (grab zone, hold depth, strafe
and let-go width) is made against the point of that line at the body's foot height, and each climbing step adds the
line's horizontal run per unit of height times the vertical speed, so the body follows it. Collision stays on as on an
upright ladder. An upright ladder's line is the vertical through its bottom: its behaviour is unchanged, and every MM6-MM8
map and every other MM9 ladder (all upright) is unaffected. `indoorLadderLeans`, `indoorLadderLinePoint` in
`IndoorMovers.cpp`.

The first one is Drangheim City's TowerLadder1 (its README): MM9's ladder leans 170 LT over its 374 LT rise through a
slot in the north wall's walkway, its foot tucked under the walkway's landing. Climbed straight up from the foot, the
party's head met that landing's underside 72 LT under the walkway, and no vertical line in the slot reaches it (the
ladder's own rails and the walkway's edges). Along the line the party clears the landing's edge about a third of the way
up. Its `top_exit` is on the walkway beside the top and it has `grab_at_top`, the way down for a party arriving by
Klusos House's window.

Survey of MM9's 68 `Ladder` volumes (rung polygons inside each volume, 2026-10-31): TowerLadder1 is the only leaning one
(rung face normal y 0.41); Lindisfarne's two lean 12 LT over 143 on an exterior map; the rest are upright or have no
rung polygons (props, ropes, poles). The shared `ladder_records` therefore keeps emitting upright ladders; a map whose
ladder leans takes its bottom and top from the rung faces, as Drangheim's `convert.py` (`DIAGONAL_LADDERS`) does.

## How the shared converter emits it

`level_generation/projects/mm9r_common/dungeon/`:

- **`movers.py`**: a new motion `rotate` for MM9's `RotatingDoor` and `Switch`: one mover per leaf (a double door's
  two leaves are two doors, one group event triggers both), pivot from `RotationPoint` and axis and angle from
  `RotationAngles`, converted with the shared frame (`native = (x, z, y) * 2.56`: the axis maps the same way; the
  frame swaps two axes, a reflection, so the angle's sign flips). The sign is then checked geometrically per kind: a
  trap's trigger half must go down, a hatch must open up into the room above, a lever's handle must move off its wall.
  (The old ODM import, `transcode_mm9_dat_to_odm.py`, maps the angles without the flip; its open-away doors hide
  that, its levers and the Isle of Ashes doors should be rechecked.) `Speed`/`ClosingSpeed` × 10 to the travel
  speeds, `Waveform` to `easing`, `OpenAway` to `open_away`, `CloseWaitTime` to `auto_close_seconds`,
  `ReopenOnContact` to `bodies: reverse`, the rule's `bodies` and `carry` for traps. It writes the mechanism
  `kind: rotating` with a `rotation` block in `geometry.yml`; `rise`, `nudge` and `slide` remain for maps that keep
  them.
- **The compiler** (`IndoorSourceGeometryCompiler.cpp`, mechanism `kind: rotating`, `extra_rooms`): the door record
  with `direction` zero and the travel in tenths of a degree; offsets are the stored pose. A rotating face stays in
  the sector list of its stored pose (floor faces in the floor list, walls in the wall list, so floor sampling, doors'
  mechanism hover and every list-based query see it as they see any face in the closed pose), takes facet type 0 so
  its kind follows its normal, and with an extra room gets it as `roomBehindNumber` and joins that room's lists.
- **`rotation.py`** (used by `movers.py` and Anskram's `convert.py`): `rotating_mover`, `swing_room` (the other room,
  from poses sampled every 15°), `ladder_records`, `grabs_at_top`, `rectangle_portals`. The `mover` block goes on the
  compiled door record in the scene (`build.py`). MM9's ladders are taken at the top, as in MM9 (2026-11-09):
  `ladder_records` writes `grab_at_top` unless the top exit lies mostly on the climber's side of the strip (a hole
  climbed out of backwards, where walking from the exit to the top reads as climbing up and the party would climb
  straight back out); an `overrides.yml` ladder's `grab_at_top: true/false` overrides that. Maps that write their own
  ladders (Drangheim City, Dark Passageway, the Training Hall but its jump-only poles) use the same `grabs_at_top`. A
  shut hatch or trapdoor over a ladder's top refuses the grab (the hold spot collides with it). A trap's or hatch's mover is not solid in the voxel pass, so its pit's air floods
  from the corridor; `rect_portals` in `overrides.yml` cut the pit off as a room at the floor plane.
- **`survey.py`** suggests `rotate` for `RotatingDoor` instead of `rise`/`nudge`.

## Continuous spinners (2026-11-06)

MM9's `RotatingBrush` (15 objects: Training Hall's horse wheels d2w1-4/d4w1-4, 1000 Terrors' ElevatorGear1/2, Dungeon
of Secrets' saw8a/b and saw9, Ruined Temple's RopeWheel, Verhoffin's SawBlade1): a model turning about one axis
through its `Pos`, one turn per `<Axis>AxisRevTime` seconds, ramping over `<Axis>AxisSpinUpTime` / `SpinDownTime`,
`<Axis>RotateForward` its sense, `StartOn` (0 for all), switched by On / Off / Toggle / Reverse messages. All but the
winch are `AttachTo` another mover: they ride it.

**Data.** A door's `mover` block may carry `spin` (on a linear door, a rotating one, or a door of no travel):

```yaml
mover:
  spin:
    pivot: [1474.6, -2068.5, 102.4]   # native, a point on the spin axis in the stored pose (the BLV's vertices)
    axis: [0.0, 0.0, 1.0]             # unit; positive turns right-handed
    degrees_per_second: 1440.0
    spin_up_seconds: 1.0              # from stopped to full speed (0: at once)
    spin_down_seconds: 4.0
    start_on: false
```

The loader refuses a spin whose axis is not a unit vector, whose speed is not above zero or whose ramps are
negative, and a spinning mover that is not rigid (as a rotating one).

**Runtime.** `RuntimeMechanismState` keeps `spinDegrees`, `spinRate` (a signed share of full speed), `spinOn`,
`spinDirection` and `spinSet` (saved, save version 161; `spinOn` takes `start_on` when the map first runs).
`IndoorSceneRuntime::advanceMoverSpins` ramps the rate toward the target (`indoorMoverSpinRateStep`: up at the
spin-up pace, down at the spin-down pace, a reversal stops first) and turns the angle every mechanism step.
`evt.SpinMover(door, "on" | "off" | "toggle" | "reverse")` switches it. The pose: `indoorRotatingMoverMotion(...,
spinDegrees)` turns the stored offsets about the pivot (moved into the offsets' frame by a linear door's full travel)
before the door's own swing or linear travel carries them, so a saw spins on its moving rail; every pose caller passes
the mechanism's angle (renderer, collision caches, movement controller, mover body rules). A spinner counts as moving
while its rate is not zero (`mechanismFacesMoving`), and its turning does not refresh the monsters' path map. Its
faces keep their stored texels and are never baked (`indoorMoverFaceBakeable`): the stored-pose light would turn with
them. Not carried: MM9's spin sounds (SpinUp/Busy/SpinDownSound) and DamagePerSecond.

**Converter.** `dungeon/movers.py` `spin_block`: a `RotatingBrush` mover gets the spin from its own properties (the
native frame mirrors LithTech's, so `RotateForward 1` is a negative native turn) and the motion of its `AttachTo`
parent (rule `motion_from` names another); motion `spin` is a spinner of no travel. The maps send MM9's messages:
Training Hall's lever (Trigger2 on + reverse, Trigger3 reverse + on, TriggerHorseGo off when the horse arrives; the
second horse's wheels get on and off in the same moment in MM9 and never turn), 1000 Terrors' lift levers (the gears
ride the lift and turn while it moves), Dungeon of Secrets' dispatcher (`saw` targets), Verhoffin's blade (on as it
comes out, off once the arm is back) and the Ruined Temple's winch (reverse + on as the prison gate opens,
SunTemplePrison.scr). Tests: `tests/IndoorMoversTests.cpp` "spinning mover: ..."; the scenario assert `mechanism`
takes `spinning` and `spin_rate_min` (`mm9r_traininghall/scenario_spin.yml`).

## Monsters and doors (a later step)

554 of MM9's 564 door leaves are `AITriggerable`: monsters open them. The converted maps open doors on the party's
click only. The user chose to leave this for a separate step: a mover flag `monsters_open: true` with which an actor
whose step is blocked by a closed face of that door triggers it. It touches the actor movement and path code.

## Implementation notes

Differences from the first design, all in the sections above: easing is for rotating movers only; `push` is not swept
against the world; rotation carries the party only and does not turn its facing; a rotating face stays in the sector
list of its stored pose instead of all three (a face in the ceiling list loses its door hover); the lightmap keeps the
UV offset for linear doors and adds `currentToStored` for rotations; ladders glide only at the top (getting on there and climbing off), are grabbed only
from the ground (or in the air with `grab_in_air`) and from their top only with `grab_at_top`, and a save on one
restores a fall. The editor writes `mover` blocks and
`ladders` back but does not preview a rotation (its viewport moves doors linearly).

**Renderer changes, all small and in `game/indoor/IndoorRenderer.cpp`** (for the next renderer work):
`mechanismFaceKeepsStoredTexels` returns true for a rotating mover; `rebuildAllTexturedBatches` builds
`rotatingFaceMotions` (empty on maps without rotating movers) and passes the face's motion to `faceLightmap`; the lightmap
UVs come from `IndoorBakedFaceLightmap::uvAt`; `updateMechanismFaceVertices` refreshes a translucent batch's `faceNormal`;
`updateMechanismRenderVerticesForDoorIds` calls the shared pose function instead of its private copy; the input code
passes Fly Up/Down to `IndoorPartyRuntime::setLadderVerticalInput`. No shader, uniform, view or draw order changed.

## Regression risks

- **The shared pose function** replaced the renderer's private copy: the same `lround` of the same float expression.
- **Lightmap selection** changes from a UV offset to composed axes: identical output for translations, checked by a
  unit test; MM6–MM8 have no sidecars, and the MM9 bakes keep their stored-pose data (format unchanged).
- **Per-step body tests** run only for movers with `bodies` other than `ignore` while they move: no cost on MM6–MM8 or
  on a still map. A push into a tight space must never leave the party inside a face (held step) or launch it
  (displacement capped at the face's own step).
- **Facet type 0** exists only on rotating faces written by the compiler.
- **Mechanism support faces** were keyed on a vertical direction; including `carry` movers must not add MM6–MM8 faces
  (they have no `mover` block).
- **Faces in two rooms** (`roomBehindNumber` on a non-portal face) already exist in MM6–MM8 data, but the converter's
  new use must not draw a leaf twice or let the pathfinder treat it as a passage.
- **Ladder input**: Fly Up/Down are read indoors only while climbing; the Android flight arrows' visibility rule
  gains one condition. Outdoors unchanged.
- **Saves**: no format change. A save of an older Anskram build (linear doors) is not migrated (unreleased map).
- **Sign of converted angles**: the converter refuses a trap whose swing does not take its trigger down or a hatch that
  does not rise. The old outdoor import, left as it is by decision, has the opposite sign: its levers swing into their
  panels (visible in Anskram's review sheet, `review/movers_compare.jpg`).
