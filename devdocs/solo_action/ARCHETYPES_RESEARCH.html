One talent path per character, nine talents a path. A melee character that lives in the pack and fights on rage, a caster that is artillery where the archer is a skirmisher. The level-728 archer is the measure of power and fun.

Arms in one line
The pack is your armour, and the fight is your fuel. Vanguard gets you in, Outnumbered and Battle Frenzy pay you for standing in the middle, Challenge brings the shooters to you, and a full rage bar is one Heroic Strike or one Demoralizing Shout.
Arcana in one line
Artillery. It stands where the archer stands and pulls as carefully, but with few large casts where the archer has many small shots. The first volley decides the fight, and the SP bar is the health bar.

How it is represented proposal

  • Rule. The path is the path tree that has ranks. Learning refuses a talent of another path tree while one has ranks. No new saved field: the saved ranks already say it.
  • Data. One line in talents.yml: paths: [arms, archery, arcana]. A ruleset that replaces the table can list none and keep free mixing.
  • Reset. As today: free, at any open trainer, clears everything. A player can change path between maps. you
  • Old saves. A character with ranks in several path trees keeps the tree with the most; the other ranks, and Shield Wall's, come back as points, as removed talents' points already do.
  • One condition stays on the Arms defences: the melee weapon is in the arms, so an Arms character shooting a bow does not carry them.

Onslaught in the Any tree you

  • Five ranks. Each kill gives 1 % speed, at most 6 / 12 / 18 / 24 / 30 stacks, lasting 60 s and refreshed by every kill, as built.
  • It shortens every recovery. Weapon attacks already read it; spell recovery does not yet and has to apply the same scale. A kill by a spell already adds a stack. code
  • Your archer keeps it through the lock. Its saved rank 3 becomes 18 % until two more points go in.
  • To check: the archer's sheet reads "Recovery time: 20". If that is a floor, more speed does nothing for it.

What else changes for your archer

  • Resolve 5: no loss. Every resistance is at the cap already.
  • Shield Wall 3: gone with the talent. It was the panic button in your battle orders at 40 % HP. Nimble, below, is the proposed Archery defence in its place.

Arms: the pack is your armour

What a melee solo faces

  • Half the bestiary shoots. 318 of 655 monster types have a missile or a spell attack. On Hell and above MAW gives pure-melee monsters a missile outdoors as well.
  • Monsters shoot from up to 5,120 units and never inside 353. Nothing makes a MAW monster hold its distance: they walk in and shoot on the way. code
  • Missiles home. A strafe (384 a second) is hit; only a run straight across the flight (768) escapes. Walking at the shooters takes every missile.
  • Armour and resistance are capped at 90 %. A late melee character sits on the cap, so only cuts applied after it count.
  • The simulation says small pulls are fine and whole groups are not. Nightmare level 500, four at a time: every melee build wins at 2 s a monster. Beyond Madness whole groups: 31 to 53 %. The model has no shooters standing off and no walking, which is where play hurts. sim
  • There is no play data for a late melee solo yet. The numbers below come from reasoning and MAW's own precedents.

Close

Getting to the pack under fire is survivable. Vanguard, Charge, Challenge.

Hold

Surrounded, you take less and hit harder. Outnumbered, Battle Frenzy.

Build

Blows and hits taken fill rage; a full bar buys the hard blow or the shout.

The risk moves; it does not go away. A melee character is in danger between places, in the sliver from 768 to 800 units where a shooter is neither counted by the pack talents nor cut by Vanguard; with few monsters around it; and against one hard hitter, where the pack talents give nothing. That last case is what Heroic Strike and the Shout are for.

Vanguardyour first idea: far missiles hurt far less

The distance is taken when the monster fires, so a Charge during the flight does not turn a far shot into a full one.

Why 800. Monsters stop shooting inside 353, and the two pack talents count within 768 (512 until you played it). That leaves only a sliver from 768 to 800 where a shooter fires at full strength and is not counted. I had proposed 512 for one radius; you kept 800. A monster's spells count as its missiles do. you

With Spell Suppression the two multiply: 60 % becomes 68 %.

Abuse. The lock keeps it from archers and casters; the melee-weapon condition keeps it from an Arms character shooting a bow. Left open: an Arms character casting with a sword out. If that shows up, the cut lapses for 3 s after the character deals spell damage.

Build. The missile has to remember how far its shooter stood (one saved number on the projectile). The hit has to say it was a missile: today a fire missile from a monster's first attack arrives looking like a melee blow. The rules have to know which weapon is drawn. Small to medium; both world runtimes touched at one call each. code

Outnumberedyour third idea: less damage per monster close

MAW has this already. Legendary 22 is 3 % per monster within 512, compounding, at most 50 %, applied after the 90 % cap. Your archer wears it. The count is computed once a frame. The talent is that legendary in ranks; with both they multiply. code

More monsters must never be safer than fewer. Ten monsters hurt like six, four like 3.4: the total still rises all the way to the cap of ten. Any retune has to keep that; the calculator below draws it.

One monster gets 4 %. A boss fight is untouched on purpose.

Battle Frenzyyour second idea, counting the monsters nearby

MAW has this too. Legendary 21: +5 % melee damage per monster within 512, at most double. The talent counts as the legendary does, but within its own 768, and Outnumbered likewise. code

Abuse: none by construction. Only a melee blow reads it, Heroic Strike's included.

Parity. In a pack of ten a swing is 375 % of a blow against the archer's 250 % per arrow. That margin pays for the time a melee character spends walking.

Challengeyour "disable projectile and spell attacks to pull ranged mobs"

A monster that cannot shoot does what the AI already does for a blinded one: it walks into melee. So Challenge turns a spread group of shooters into the pack that Cleave, Sweeping Blows, Outnumbered and Battle Frenzy all want. Bosses: half the time.

Build. Blind and Dark Grasp already force a monster into melee. A third timer of the same kind in both runtimes, and spell rolls that respect it. Medium. code

The shared floor. Everything the solo run cuts (Vanguard, Outnumbered, the Shout, Spell Suppression, Unbroken's part, and Nimble and Phase on the other paths) should together never leave less than 20 % of a hit. With Shield Wall gone and Vanguard at 60 %, it takes Vanguard, Spell Suppression and ten monsters under Outnumbered together to reach it (19 %); the floor is there for what is added later.

Before any of it: Cleave has to be trustworthy. These talents multiply what Cleave and Sweeping Blows do, and the report in your thread says they sometimes strike one monster in a crowd with no sign why. They should say what they struck, and Cleave should never strike fewer monsters than an ordinary swing with Sweeping Blows would have.

What a full bar buys

  • Heroic Strike, no cooldown. One blow at 200 / 250 / 300 % of a normal blow on the target, in half a swing's recovery, as Cleave and Power Shot are. You set 300; I read the lower ranks as 200 and 250. At rank 3 it adds about 30 % to the damage on its target in a pack (one every 5 s) and about 13 % on a boss (one every 11 s). It is the Arms answer to Power Shot (200 % every 4 s or less) and the single-target tool the pack talents do not give.
  • Demoralizing Shout, cooldown 45 / 40 / 35 s. Hostile monsters within 1,024 deal 30 % less damage for 6 / 8 / 10 s. MAW already has this cut at this size: a monster under Shrinking Ray or Dark Grasp deals 30 % less, "together not more". The Shout sets the same flag, so it does not stack with those two. It acts on the monster's damage before your mitigation, so it counts in full for a character at the 90 % cap. code
  • The choice at a full bar is damage or defence: the strike now, or the shout if it is off its cooldown.

Where it shows and where it hooks

  • An arc round the right side of the portrait in the solo unit frame, red while it fills and gold when full (your sketch; it was a third bar under SP at first). The two abilities' action-bar slots are veiled until then.
  • The rules already have every number at the right place: the party's hit on a monster carries the damage, the monster, whether it was melee, an extra blow or a kill, next to where Onslaught counts its kill; the damage a character takes is known where it is absorbed; the talents' update already knows "out of combat". code
  • Data: a rage: block in talents.yml with the five numbers above, and rage_cost: 100 on the two talents. Heroic Strike is otherwise Power Shot's fields.
  • The Shout and Challenge each need one new monster state in both world runtimes, so they are best built together.

Breach, in Shatter's place

You turned Shatter down: monsters are held or slowed for a few seconds in twenty, so it would not have much effect. Breach is always on and needs no set-up. MAW halves a spell's damage for every 100 points of the monster's resistance, so counting less of that resistance is worth: code

  • It is largest exactly where a caster is stuck: against the monsters it cannot hurt today, and nothing against the ones it already kills. Resistance is also why the Dragon, whose breath ignores it, is the strongest solo class.
  • Small to build: one factor on the resistance where the monster's mitigation is computed. No state, no timer.
  • If not this: a spell cast after standing still for a second hits 5 / 10 / 15 % harder (the artillery plants its feet; the mirror of Steady Aim), or your Expose Weakness for spell crits.

Phase makes the escape an escape: a Blink out of a pack is followed by whatever was already in flight. Small to build. You did not object, so I take it as in.

The three damage talents add; they do not multiply. Spell Surge (+50 %), Overload (up to +90 %) and Opening Volley (+45 %) together are at most +185 %: a little under three times a plain cast, where multiplying would give 4.1 times. MAW's developer avoids multiplicative stacks. Breach is not in that sum: it acts on the monster's resistance, before them.

Chaining

  • Arc is Chaining Arrows for bolts, at the same 1,024. The cheap spell on the Attack key becomes an area attack, as the archer's ordinary shot is, and every hit feeds Spell Surge. MAW's Chain Lightning already hops, and the arrows' chain was modelled on it, so there is code on both sides to start from. code
  • Overload, fixed, makes every area spell count the pack it hits. That is the talent the simulation valued most for casters and the game barely has.

Survivability

Why a mage dies, in the simulation's numbers at level 500 (its gear was poor, so read the proportions): 8.1k HP, which is 40 % of what MAW expects, and 8,150 SP. Mana Shield at Grandmaster absorbs every hit at 5 HP for each SP, so the SP bar is worth about 41k HP, more than a Champion's 34k. But the mage takes 33 to 44 % of a magic hit where the Champion takes 10 %, nothing regenerates SP the way your archer regenerates HP, and the same bar pays for the spells. sim

  1. Kill first. Opening Volley, a surged area spell and Overload: most of a pack should not survive to shoot back. This is the layer that fits the identity, and it is what "pull carefully" pays for.
  2. Do not be reached. Blink and Binding Circle, as built, and Phase for the seconds after the jump.
  3. Absorb what lands. Mana Shield with Arcane Ward (5 HP per SP becomes 7.1, the bar worth about 58k), Manathirst to refill it from hits, Spell Suppression, and the two keystones.

Arcane Ward does not make the mage a tank: with its resistances it still has about half a Champion's staying power. It makes one mistake survivable.

Not known: how a real late mage's sheet reads. There is no sorcerer save, and the simulation's kits were weak. Reading one at your archer's level is the first step.

How a pull plays

Spell Surge ready→ The opener on a pack that has not moved: free, with Opening Volley, Surge and Overload→ Bolts on Attack; Arc spreads them, each hit a second off the next Surge→ Binding Circle when they arrive→ Blink out, Phase covering the jump

Kills stack Onslaught, so the casts quicken through a long fight. By class: the Elementalist's stacks already reward casting without a pause; the Priest stays the weakest and needs its own answer; the fixed Overload makes the breath Dragon stronger, so check it first when simulating.

Parity: when are the three level?

At the same level and gear tier the three paths should clear the same map in about the same time and die about as often, with the danger in different places (the last row of the identities table).

Three ways to measure, cheapest first

  1. MAW's own sheet. It already prints Power and Vitality. Your archer: Ranged Power 564K, Vitality 390K. Read a melee and a caster character of level 728 with gear of the same tier against those two numbers before tuning any talent. If a sheet is far below, talents are the wrong place to fix it.
  2. The simulation. Its talent model has range (groups start 1,200 units away, shooters stand off at 600). Add the Arms talents and rage, the fixed Overload, the fed Spell Surge, Arc and Opening Volley, and rerun whole groups on Nightmare 300 and 500 and Beyond Madness 500 beside the Archer.
  3. Play. One dimension map of your archer's tier (your quicksave sits in one: power 47, tier 490) with a melee and a caster test character made at its level.

First targets for play, against the archer on the same map: clear time within 25 %; no more deaths; a hand on an active every 3 to 6 s; the largest single hit below 90 % of the bar.
