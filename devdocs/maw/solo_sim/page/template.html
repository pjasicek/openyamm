MAW Solo Classes

Which classes can play MAW alone, at character levels 10, 50, 200 and 500, on every difficulty from Normal to Beyond Madness. Every number comes from OpenYAMM's own MAW code. A simulated solo playthrough clears the real MM6–MM8 maps (their spawned monsters, chests, completion rewards and quests); each build then wears the best gear that playthrough's loot offers, crafted with the materials it found, and fights the monsters of the maps it has reached. The key formulas were checked against live headless game runs.

The answer

Ranked by how much of the real maps each build handles at each stage: the actual monster groups of the maps the simulated playthrough is clearing, pulled four at a time without potions, with the class the character has by then and gear from that playthrough's loot. "Nightmare 50 / 200 / 500" is the share of those maps' monsters it beats; Doom, Road to Insanity and Beyond Madness are given at level 500, where they separate the builds. The generic pack test further down (B-tier melee monsters all attacking at once) gives the same order.

Best solo tier 1

  • Great Wyrm (Dragon). 100 % at every Nightmare stage and at level 500 in Doom and Road to Insanity, 84 % in Beyond Madness, the best of all; the fastest clears (under 1 s per monster). Breath hits an area and takes the monster's lowest resistance, and dragons count every item stat three times. Even a level-10 Dragon beats everything around it.
  • Archangel (Seraph), two-handed sword + shield. 100 % on Nightmare from level 50, 97 % (Doom) and 89 % (Road to Insanity) at level 500, 47 % in Beyond Madness, second best there. Spirit cuts every hit to its 25 % floor and Body heals it on every blow. Part of this comes from an OpenYAMM port error (below); it stays near the top either way.

Strong tier 2

  • War Troll. 100 % at level 500 in Doom and Road to Insanity and 57 % in Beyond Madness, on 8–15 % of its HP regenerated per second, but the slowest killer: about 9 s per monster at Nightmare L500 and far more in Beyond Madness.
  • Marabou (Shaman) in melee: 100 % on Nightmare from level 50, 90 % (Doom) and 72 % (Road to Insanity) at level 500; falls apart in Beyond Madness (3 %). Same port-error caveat as the Seraph.
  • Minotaur Lord (two axes) and the Troll-race Champion: 100 % on Nightmare at every stage from 50, 89–92 % (Doom) and 63–69 % (Road to Insanity) at level 500; the best plain melee.

Workable with pulls tier 3

  • Knights: Vampire-race Black Knight and Human Champion, 95–100 % on Nightmare, 80–84 % in Doom and 54–61 % in Road to Insanity at level 500.
  • Death Knights (Lich King, Vampire race one axe or Minotaur race two axes), Nosferatu, Monk: 90–100 % on Nightmare but only 32–56 % at level 500 in Doom and Road to Insanity.
  • Master Archer does better in real groups than in packs (91–94 % on Nightmare, about half in Doom and Road to Insanity at level 500).
  • Casters (Arch Mage, Elementalist): fine around level 50–200 (80–92 % on Nightmare), 69–77 % at Nightmare L500 and 11–22 % in Doom and Road to Insanity.

Not viable avoid

  • Priest of Light (Cleric): 46 / 47 / 19 % on Nightmare, 2–4 % at level 500 in Doom and Road to Insanity, and the slowest kills.
  • Assassin (Thief line): 86 / 82 / 80 % on Nightmare but 25–38 % at level 500 in Doom and Road to Insanity; no Armsmaster in MAW, 5 HP per level.

Early game: at level 10 on Nightmare and harder, before any promotion, most builds still need potions or careful pulls (a plain Knight beats only 27 % of the groups around it on Nightmare, 4 % on Road to Insanity); the Dragon, Seraph, Troll and Shaman are the exceptions. From level 50 the first promotion and gear carry everyone but the Cleric.

okMinotaur knight

middleMinotaur death knight with axes

paperCasters

Good from about level 50 to 200 on Nightmare (Grandmaster Mana Shield absorbs hits at up to 5 HP per spell point), weak at L500 where one hit takes most of their 3–6 k HP. avoidCleric

Confirmed not viable: the slowest kills and the worst survival of all 22 builds, in real groups and in packs.

Why the Seraph is this strong

Two class mechanics, Spirit and Body, both of which a solo gets in full; together they carry a character with a third of a Knight's HP. Numbers from the simulated Nightmare playthrough.

Spirit: most hits land at 25 %

After armour every hit is cut by a flat amount, never below 25 % of the hit: max(hit − flat, 0.25·hit). The flat amount is sized for an average party member at the level bank: about the bank monster's hit before armour × your Spirit skill ÷ that member's expected skill. It assumes average armour, and a solo is about twice the bank level (L200: bank 99, Spirit 27 against an expected 19), so the cut exceeds the whole hit and every hit falls to its floor: a permanent 75 % reduction, physical and magic alike.

Body: a big heal on every blow

Each melee hit heals the most wounded member by Body^1.3 × mastery × 2 × speed factor × (1 + Personality/1000). A party shares it; a solo is always the one healed. It grows faster than the Seraph's 3–4 HP per level, and Light speeds the swings (one per 0.5 s at L200), so it heals about twice a second. The heal does not depend on the damage dealt or the monster's resistances.

  • Together, at L200 one monster takes about 1 % of its HP per second and the heals return 42 % per second: around 40 monsters hitting at once before it loses ground. The Champion has more HP and no healing, so damage just accumulates.
  • Where it breaks is the low HP: the floor still lets the biggest hits through and the heal comes between swings. In Beyond Madness L500 a B hit lands for 1,248 (16 % of its 7,977 HP) and a room of C-tier casters deals more than its HP in a second; it beats 47 % of the real groups there.
  • OpenYAMM's flat cut leaves out two factors MAW includes (the expected chance to be hit and the legendary damage-taken factor), so it is 25–40 % larger. Up to Road to Insanity both versions exceed the hit and the floor binds anyway; in Beyond Madness L500 MAW's formula would land 2,692 per hit instead of 1,248. The Shaman's Water cut uses the same formula.
  • Divine Protection (once every five minutes, saved from a killing blow) is not simulated.

Why a solo works at all

Monsters are not scaled down for you

Every monster's HP and damage are sized against one expected character of the monster's own level: a B-tier monster takes 1 (Easy) to 5 (Beyond Madness) of that character's attacks and kills it in 14 down to 4 hits. Party size changes nothing except Cover. A party is five such characters; a solo is one.

What a solo gets back is experience. Each kill banks the awarded experience divided by 5, whatever the party size, and the level bank sets the monsters' levels. A solo member receives the whole award, five times a party member's share. Quests and map completion pay × 5 / party size too. A kill's experience does shrink against easy monsters, but MAW measures that against the levels banked (monster HP over the HP a monster of the bank's level has), not against your character, so a solo far above its bank loses nothing extra (zzzMaw_Experience.lua:192-205, the same in OpenYAMM).

  • Group sizes per spawn: MAW 1–3, Hard and Hell 1–4, Nightmare 2–4, Doom 2–5, Road to Insanity 3–5, Beyond Madness 3–6. A "pack of 4" is a normal Nightmare pull; 10 is two or three groups at once.
  • Solo rules that help: Identify, Repair, Merchant, Perception, Disarm, Identify Monster and Alchemy reach Expert, Master and Grandmaster at skill 4, 7 and 10 whatever the class; Cover halves a hit instead of passing it on; poison and disease are halved; crowd control lasts twice as long.
  • The solo's one hard limit: if its only member is paralysed, stoned or knocked unconscious, the game is over. Protection from Magic (MAW keeps it at 50 charges; the Magic Protection potion provides it) blocks paralysis, stone and death. Keep it up against basilisks, gorgons, medusas, cyclopes and gargoyles. A solo playthrough, simulated

A fresh MAW game per difficulty, five times with different seeds (medians shown). The solo always clears the uncleared MM6–MM8 map whose monsters are lowest at that moment, killing every monster the map spawns on that difficulty (MAW's density and bosses, read from headless map loads), opening every chest, taking the completion reward and its quests. Experience, corpse loot, chest loot and crafting materials all come from the game's own code. The rows show where the character passes each level.

  • A full clear of the 189 maps ends at level 486–498 on Normal to Hell, 503 on Nightmare, about 470 in Doom and Road to Insanity and 804 in Beyond Madness. A first clear reaches level 500 only on Nightmare (at its very end) and in Beyond Madness; elsewhere the level-500 column shows the character at the end of its full clear (level 469–498), and level 500 itself needs map resets or dimension maps.
  • The monsters at each point are lower than my first estimate (about 16 / 90 / 235 at L50 / 200 / 500 in free progression, against 23 / 99 / 275), because kills, quests and map completion all pay a solo five times a party member's share while the monster bank grows as for a party. Even with no quests at all a level-50 solo meets level 14–20 monsters.
  • Quests: 196 events that give experience, counting one reward per event because the branches of one quest are alternatives (9.4 M before MAW's bonus), handed out smallest first along the route. MAW pays each value·(1 + P/100) + 500·P, P being the levels banked on the other continents, so late quests are worth a lot: they are 14–26 % of the experience up to level 200 in free progression, about half in Doom and Road to Insanity, and about half by the end of a Nightmare clear. Without quests a Nightmare full clear stops at level 379.

The four stages

At Nightmare: where the playthrough is when the character reaches each level, and what it has to build with.

Skill points: 5 + ⌊level/10⌋ per level. Raising a skill to s costs s; a second weapon or school at the same level costs half, a third a third (Seraph, Shaman, Death Knight and Assassin share only their schools). Gear: for each slot, the item from that run's loot that makes the build strongest (its damage × MAW's vitality, leech counted); then the run's Emeralds, Hourglasses, Eyes of Void, gems, Pandora's Cubes and Crafting Cubes applied with the game's crafting code. Potion power: best map reagent (drop level = content level / 3, base 30) plus the Grandmaster Alchemy level.

Real map groups

The packs above are generic: B-tier melee monsters with no resistances. Here every build fights the actual groups of the maps it is clearing at that stage: the last five maps the playthrough cleared and the next five it would take, each monster with its own tier, level, HP, attacks, spells, damage types, recovery and resistances at that moment. A group is the monsters standing within 512 units of each other, as the map spawns them on that difficulty. The class is the one the character has by then (see the notes below the table).

Each cell is the share of those maps' monsters that stand in groups the build beats (a pull of 4 resting to full between pulls, or the whole group at once). Hover for the class, clear time and potions.

  • Monster damage: attack 1 is the map's MAW value for the monster's own row; attack 2 scales by its dice against attack 1; a spell from the player schools deals the monster's magic damage times MAW's per-spell multiplier (Fire Bolt 0.75, Fireball 0.5, Lightning Bolt 0.95, Mind Blast 0.8, Psychic Shock 1.25 …). Each monster casts spell 1 at its chance, then spell 2, then attack 2, else attack 1; buffs and curses cost it the turn. Missiles and spells roll the Speed dodge twice. Bosses: their HP as spawned, damage × 1.3 (MAW's average).
  • Your hits are cut by the monster's resistance to their type (÷ 2^(resistance/100), capped at 200); a dragon's breath takes its lowest resistance. You kill the monster with the most damage per HP first.
  • Class at each stage: the base class until 15 % of one continent's maps are cleared, the first promotion until 40 % of one, then the final class; Elder (× 1.25 HP and SP per level) at two continents past 40 %, Ultimate (× 1.5) at three. Skill caps, masteries and spells follow that class and the difficulty's mastery thresholds; a spell the character cannot cast yet is not used. Promotion quests are not simulated: the 15 % and 40 % marks stand in for doing them along the way.
  • Not modelled: special attacks (paralysis, stone, disease), monsters' buffs, terrain and doors, line of sight and kiting. Fights are expected values, so a lucky or unlucky run can differ.

One stage in detail

This is the harsh generic test: 4 or 10 identical B-tier monsters, all hitting from the first second, no walking in, no actives. "With talent tree v2" adds everything this test can hold: Grandmaster Bodybuilding (Lone Discipline), the passives, the Oath, Unbroken, Archon, Sweeping Blows, Overload, Bloodthirst and Mana Font. Range and the actives (Cleave, Frenzy, Charge, Shield Wall, Blink, Binding Circle, Disengage, Multishot) need the real-group model; see "Talent tree v2 for the solo run" above.

DPS is against one B-tier monster of the content level: melee from MAW's own attack resolver (hit chance, crits, enchantments, class add-ons), bow from MAW's power report, breath per target, spells at full mana (sustained figure after it). Incoming is per monster, per second, as a share of your HP, after Speed dodge, armour, class cuts and the 25 % floor; "magic" is the same for an elemental caster. Sustain is regeneration (Grandmaster at half HP) plus the Regeneration potion; leech and on-hit heals are in the fights but not in this column. Packs: clear time and lowest HP reached, without potions; "drinks" is Divine Cures needed when drinking below 35 %. The packs hit only physically, so a build whose defence is weak against magic looks better here than in the real maps: in Beyond Madness at L500 a monster's magic hit (71 k) is about twice its physical one, and 74 % of the damage in the real maps is magic. The Seraph's flat cut still floors physical hits there (1,248 lands, 16 % of its HP) but not magic ones (6.7 k lands, 84 % of its HP), which is why the real-group section ranks the Dragon first. HP spreads widely because Death Knights and Seraphim stop at Expert Bodybuilding while Minotaurs, Trolls and Dragons reach Grandmaster.

Gear and potions for a solo

Buff potions are your party

  • A buff potion casts its aura on the drinker as a Master caster with skill 50·(f − 1) and level 0.5·P·f, where f = 0.75 + 0.75·min(P, 200)/200. Below power 34 the skill is negative.
  • Haste and Heroism stop improving at power 200; Stone Skin, Bless and the resistances keep growing with the potion's level.
  • Brew Champion's Potion (Haste, Heroism, Bless), Paladin's Potion (Shield, Stone Skin, Preservation), Elemental and Self Resistance, and from Master Alchemy Divine Blessing (Day of the Gods).
  • Pure Power, Wisdom and Resilience add 20 to their stats per 50 power, once per step.

Healing potions fall behind

  • Cure Wounds heals 20 + 25 %, Divine Cure 50 + 35 % of the HP a level-P character is expected to have. A solo has 2–5 times that, so at L200 a Divine Cure restores 7 % of a Minotaur's HP and 35 % of an Arch Mage's.
  • The Regeneration potion heals √maxHP · ⌊P/2⌋^1.25 · 0.005 per second, 0.5–1 % of HP per second for big-HP classes and up to about 3 % for casters.
  • Leech is the healing that scales: a blow heals (damage ÷ a B monster's HP) × leech share × your max HP, at least share/5 × recovery/100 of max HP per hit. Vampiric and Darkness weapons add 10 % melee leech to anyone.
  • Dragons triple every item stat; Crafting Cubes add 2 power per cube to weapons and body armour (4 to the rest) up to ⌊your level / 5⌋.
  • The portrait limits the slots: dragon portraits wear only rings, an amulet and gauntlets; Minotaur portraits wear no helm and no boots. The simulation applies these rules (the game's doll types).

What I found along the way

A port error that flatters Seraphs and Shamans

MAW sizes the Seraph's Spirit and the Shaman's Water flat cut with the expected character's damage share including its chance to be hit and its legendary reduction (local _,_,_,avgTaken = getPlayerEstimatedVitality(lvl+1), zzMaw-Stats.lua:754; Estimates.lua:448). OpenYAMM's mawClassDefense (MawClassRules.cpp, near line 837) uses (physical + magic) / 2 without those two factors, so its cut is about 1.3 times MAW's at level 100 and 1.6 times at level 275. It has been like this since the first port (MAW changed it before ef23ee17). With MAW's formula the Seraph's packs at L500 Nightmare drop from about 20 to 16; the ranking does not change.

A correction to this page (2026-11-14)

An earlier version gave every build with Body magic the Seraph's and the Shaman's on-hit heals; the game gives each only to its own class line. Death Knights (whose Blood is Body magic), Shamans and the Priest were overrated and are corrected here.

Corrections to the 2026-11-13 solo study

  • Death Knights cannot learn Armsmaster; its Death Knight build used it as a core skill.
  • Reagents drop at a third of the level, not half, so solo potion powers are about 32 / 49 / 97 / 193 at these stages, not 100–375.
  • Its "Monk" was measured with a staff (the test party picks the best weapon). Bare-handed, a Monk deals a flat 162 at L200; the staff is MAW's Monk weapon.
  • The Stats page's Melee Power reads about 40 % below the real attack for the dual-dagger Assassin; I did not trace why.

Limits

  • Stand and trade: every monster in the pack attacks from the first second, and there is no kiting, terrain, crowd control or first strike from range. Ranged builds that open from range do better (3 free seconds lift the L200 Nightmare Arch Mage from 5 to 10 monsters and the L500 Dragon from 4 to 15).
  • In the generic pack test all monsters are B-tier with physical melee attacks and monster recovery 80 (one attack per 1.4 s), without resistances. The real-group section uses each monster's own tier, attacks, spells, recovery and resistances instead (A-tier hit for 0.66, C-tier for 1.5; bosses have their spawned HP and 1.3 times the damage).
  • The playthrough: easiest map first, every map cleared once, no map resets, dimension maps, Arena or bounties; quests paid smallest first along the route rather than where they are given. Shops are not used (their stock could only add gear), and the Mirror, Pearl and the Orbs are not spent.
  • Gear is chosen per slot by the build's damage × MAW's vitality (leech counted, casters' mana weighted); immunity specials (of Freedom, of the Medusa) are worth more to a solo than that measure says.
  • Conditions are not modelled; see the Protection from Magic note.
  • The builds use one sensible skill split each, not an exhaustive optimisation. Death Knight spells, Shaman spells, the Assassin's combo finishers and the Seraph's Divine Protection are not used, so those classes are slightly undersold. Elementalist stacks are held at 5.
  • Class progression: the base class until 15 % of a continent's maps are cleared, then the first promotion, the final class at 40 %; Elder and Ultimate (HP and SP per level × 1.25 and × 1.5) from two and three continents past 40 %. The promotion quests themselves are not played; Chedian's fourth promotion (Supreme) is not reached because MM9 is not in the route.
  • This is plain MAW solo (a party of one). OpenYAMM's optional augmented solo run with talents (Cleave, Whirlwind, Shield Wall…) is not included.
