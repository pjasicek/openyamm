Asset: a flat single-picture surface texture for a high-resolution remaster of Might and Magic IV: Clouds of Xeen (1992), used in a 3D engine like
that of Might and Magic VI-VIII.
The reference images show the original 1992 VGA art (small pixel art shown enlarged) and a smoothed layout guide made
from it: the half-timbered houses of the town of Vertigo: smooth pale peach-pink lime plaster with faint hairline cracks and a fine stippled grain, in panels framed by squared oak beams of a warm mid reddish brown; clean, bright and cheerful, the original's own colours kept exactly.
Repaint it at high resolution as a modern remaster would: keep the original's design, layout, proportions and colours,
and add the believable material detail its low resolution could not show (one texel is about one centimetre), in a
hand-painted, slightly stylised realism, not a photograph. No pixel-art look, no dithering, no hard pixel steps.
Flat orthographic front view, even diffuse light, no perspective, no vignette, no baked highlights, glare or cast
shadows, no watermark or signature.
Composition: full bleed, the subject fills the whole 1024x1536 (portrait) canvas edge to edge. One wing (the left leaf) of the original's double door, laid out exactly as Reference 1 (the layout guide, the left half of the door in Reference 2): upright dark brown oak planks; two heavy horizontal battens studded with rows of small brass rivets; two long polished brass strap hinges along the left (hinge) edge, reaching in across the battens; between the battens a recessed plank panel framed by a thin brass bead; a small brass handle at the right edge at half height. The right edge is the plain plank edge where the other wing meets it. The same warm dark brown oak and brass as the door of Reference 3 (a finished door of the set). Only the door leaf, edge to edge: no wall, no plaster, no posts round it.
Every edge row and column is the material itself: no dark outline, frame line or vignette along the canvas edges.
