Asset: a flat single-picture surface texture for a high-resolution remaster of Might and Magic IV: Clouds of Xeen (1992), used in a 3D engine like
that of Might and Magic VI-VIII.
The reference images show the original 1992 VGA art (small pixel art shown enlarged) and a smoothed layout guide made
from it: the half-timbered houses of the town of Vertigo, aged a few decades: warm off-white lime plaster with a faint peach tint, soft weathering, faint grime near the bottom and hairline cracks, in panels framed by dark weathered oak beams with visible grain, wooden pegs at the joints and drying checks; the original's colours a little darker and less saturated, to sit beside the half-timbered town of Harmondale in Might and Magic VII.
Repaint it at high resolution as a modern remaster would: keep the original's design, layout, proportions and colours,
and add the believable material detail its low resolution could not show (one texel is about one centimetre), in a
hand-painted, slightly stylised realism, not a photograph. No pixel-art look, no dithering, no hard pixel steps.
Flat orthographic front view, even diffuse light, no perspective, no vignette, no baked highlights, glare or cast
shadows, no watermark or signature.
Composition: full bleed, the subject fills the whole 1024x1536 (portrait) canvas edge to edge. One bay of a half-timbered wall, laid out exactly as Reference 1 (the layout guide): a vertical squared oak post runs the full height along the left edge and another along the right edge, each cut in half lengthwise by the canvas edge, so bays placed side by side meet in one whole post; a horizontal oak beam runs the full width along the top edge and another along the bottom edge; between them one tall plaster panel fills the rest, slightly recessed behind the timber. Reference 2 is the original wall of three such panels; Reference 3 is a finished Might and Magic VII wall whose level of detail and weathering to match (not its layout). No window, door, torch or other object on the panel.
Every edge row and column is the material itself: no dark outline, frame line or vignette along the canvas edges.
