Asset: a flat single-picture surface texture for Might and Magic VI-VIII (a 1990s first-person fantasy RPG), in the remaster's high-resolution art.
The reference images are finished textures of the same place in the game: the brown-grey cave rock of Might and Magic VII's Nighon Tunnels (cwb1 and its gold-vein variant cwb1gold): rough fractured rock in warm grey-brown, deep dark cracks, hand-painted.
Paint the new texture as if by the same artists for the same set: the same palette, saturation and contrast, the same
scale of detail (one texel is about one centimetre), the same hand-painted, slightly stylised realism (painted, not a
photograph), the same soft even lighting. Flat orthographic front view, even diffuse light, no perspective, no
vignette, no baked highlights, glare or cast shadows, no watermark or signature.
Composition: full bleed, the subject fills the whole 1024x1024 (square) canvas edge to edge. A great stern face carved in low relief out of the gold-veined cave rock of the first reference, as if the rock itself had grown a face: a broad brow, a heavy straight nose, a wide closed mouth and a long beard of rock that flows down into the stone, the features shaped from the same warm grey-brown fractured rock with its veins of raw gold running across the cheeks, brow and beard. Its two eyes are large faceted sapphire crystals set into the rock, deep clear royal blue with paler blue facets and a soft inner glow. The face fills the middle of the picture; all round it, out to every edge, the plain gold-veined rock of the first reference continues unchanged, the same colours, cracks, grain and scale, so the face sits in the wall without any frame, border or edge line. The relief is shaded by soft even light only, ancient and dignified, not a monster, not a mask, not a poster.
Every edge row and column is the material itself: no dark outline, frame line or vignette along the canvas edges.
