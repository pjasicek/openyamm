# Foliage paintover brief

The attached image is an in-game screenshot of New Sorpigal, a village from Might and Magic VI, running in a
modern remake engine. Produce ONE edited version of this exact screenshot with your built-in image generation tool:
a concept paintover that shows how the ground foliage should look. It is an art-direction target for a real-time
game, not a photo.

## Keep exactly

- Camera, framing, horizon, terrain shape, every building, wall, road, cobbled path, tree and its position.
- The HUD (top-left bar, portraits, minimap, bottom portrait frame) unchanged, pixel for pixel where possible.
- The stylised, slightly painterly look of the game's trees and buildings. Do NOT turn it photoreal.
- Paths, roads, the town square and doorways stay walkable and clear: bare dirt or cobbles, at most a thin grassy
  verge along their edges.

## Change: the ground vegetation, layered like a real meadow

1. Base layer: the meadow is a continuous, soft mat of short fine grass whose colour matches the ground beneath it
   (no bright yellow tufts sitting on a darker lawn). From above it reads as varied grass, not as dots.
2. Variation by patch, not by plant: broad soft patches of slightly taller uncut grass with pale seed heads, and
   lower, greener lawn between them. Patches are tens of metres across with irregular, natural edges.
   Slightly drier, yellower grass on hilltops and exposed slopes; darker, lusher green in hollows and near water.
3. Unmown edges: taller grass and weeds grow along stone walls, fences, building bases, rocks and path verges, as
   they do in the countryside.
4. Undergrowth grounds the trees: low bushes, ferns and taller grass around many tree trunks and under canopies,
   with a few free-standing shrubs between trees, so trees no longer stand on a bare lawn.
5. Wildflowers in small single-colour clusters (yellow, white, a little purple), not sprinkled evenly.
6. Scale: the base grass is ankle height for a person; tall patches reach the knee at most; bushes are waist high.

Keep the overall colour temperature and time of day of the original. Save the result as a PNG with the file name
given in the task, in the same folder as this brief. Generate once; regenerate at most once if the HUD, framing or
buildings changed. Then stop.
