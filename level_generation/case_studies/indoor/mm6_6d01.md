## Layout and visual reading

Long curved cave arms flank a compact architectural complex. The plan combines broad chambers, narrow connectors and small regular rooms. This early-game dungeon already has dozens of sectors and doors: apparent gameplay simplicity is not a reliable geometry budget.

transparency is not composited in Blender; magenta edges are a preview limitation. Neither image is a game screenshot.

## Decoration, architecture and terrain study

Repeated torches track the built corridors and thresholds while the chambers retain open floors. Structural wall recesses and the contrast between cave and built materials carry much of the visual variety. Place lights in relation to turns and doors, with sparse room-specific props.

This reading is an interpretation of the measured arrangement, not a claim about the original authors’ documented intent.

## Apply to a new map

Separate the natural circulation graph from the built puzzle module. Predeclare switches, their affected door groups, texture feedback and encounter side effects. Do not give every wall facet its own sector or collapse the entire complex into one room.

## Follow-up review

Test puzzle failure and success, repeat activation, ambush clearance, all return routes and visibility through the cave-to-building transitions. Use a close cutaway of the central complex; the full-map view makes its controls too small to judge.

Scope runtime checks to new mechanisms or unresolved behavior using the [review policy](../../REVIEW_CHECKLIST.md).

These are review suggestions, not gameplay tests executed by this survey.
