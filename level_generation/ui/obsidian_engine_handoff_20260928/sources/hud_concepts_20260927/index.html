Buff capacity: 22 party effects, 10 per-character effect types +

The strip counts active party effects. Personal effects stay with their portrait. Day of Protection contributes eight party effects; Hour of Power adds up to four more plus personal Bless. The 12-effect preset reflects that case. “22 · all slots” is a capacity test, not a normal casting sequence.

Default: four soonest to expire, then +N for the hidden count. The alternative shows up to eight effects in two rows, followed by the same overflow control. Both stay within a 268-pixel-wide area at 1600 × 900. Equal durations keep their order. The list is held still while a buff is being inspected.

Static shading is the default for bars. Play damage demonstrates a short delayed damage trail; the optional pulse is confined to critical HP. Reduced-motion preferences disable the motion. The green, amber and red alert sigils retain the existing readiness/danger meaning; recovery and incapacity use a dark, unlit treatment.

Source: Party.h and PartySpellSystem.cpp. Refinement notes and full buff list.

What changes, what stays, and where the art comes from +

The present HUD

The game already has a useful layout: a party-sized basebar, oval faces, HP/SP columns, three utility buttons, separate gold and food strips, two buff tableaux, a minimap and followers. The heavy repeated green frames, small indicators and unevenly scattered groups make those pieces compete.

The aim is clearer hierarchy and more coherent materials. The portraits remain the visual anchor.

The same game underneath

Every direction keeps portrait selection and inspection, health, spell points, recovery, conditions, the danger/readiness indicator, party and personal buffs, gold, food, resting, books, quick reference, options, followers and map zoom. Turn-based attack, movement and waiting states have a reserved indicator.

Inventory, dialogue, spellbook, journal and other full screens retain their existing designs. Buttons here show a small handoff notice.

A proposal you can inspect

The faces, resource icons, spell artwork and MM6 map are unmodified local assets. Obsidian adds six generated icons for commands, followers and personal buffs. The frame, layout and typography are browser-drawn proposals. All three directions use the same cropped gameplay view from test_img/2000.png; the Current tab shows the untouched image.

Party names, health, durations, map position and status examples are illustrative. Dusk is a simulated contrast check. Original asset sources · New icons and prompts · Design and implementation notes
