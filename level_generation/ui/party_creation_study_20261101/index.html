Party creation — three approaches

Interactive Carved Obsidian mockups for a full-screen OpenYAMM party creation screen, built on the real MMerge tables (classes, caps, promotions, race rules, portraits, voices) for the Standard and MAW rulesets, with the real voice lines playable. Each page renders the game's 853⅓×480 menu canvas and can simulate a 4:3 window and a 6.5″ phone (touch input, 9 mm fingertip). Serve the repository root (python3 -m http.server 8917) and open the pages; the review bar switches approach, rules, continent, view and party.

Vanilla MM7 heritage: all five adventurers side by side with portrait, class, voice, racial line, attributes and starting skills. One shared strip below shows the selected column's full skill list (40 chips) with party markers.

B · Skill board

The "skill bingo" is the screen: skills as rows, the five members as columns, every cell showing what that member starts with, can choose, learns later or after promotion. A one-member editor sits on the left.

C · Focus + rail

One large character sheet (portrait, class, voice card, race card, attributes) with a roster rail of five cards, and the whole skill list as wide rows below. Party board opens approach B's board as an overlay.

Open C4:3phoneMAWboard overlay

Recommendation: C, with B's board as its "Party board" overlay

Build approach C as the screen and keep approach B's board as a full-screen overlay one button away (already wired in c.html). C reads best: one adventurer at a time, with racial traits, voice and mastery spelled out in full sentences and the complete skill list always visible with party markers. It has the largest controls of the three, so it degrades least on Android and on a letterboxed 4:3 window, and it keeps the current NewGameScreen model (one edited m_state plus m_partyStates), so it is the cheapest to build. The board covers what C lacks — whole-party planning — without making it the default for players who just want a party.

Choose A instead if the MM7 look of five full columns matters most. It is the most authentic and shows every attribute at once, but its seventy small stepper buttons and 1.7 mm median targets on a phone make it the least comfortable, and drawing five live editors needs per-slot versions of the stat and skill logic.

Phone numbers: every interactive element measured on a 6.5″ 20:9 phone (the 480-unit canvas is 67.7 mm tall, 0.141 mm per unit; smallTargetAudit() in common.js, recorded in validation.json). No layout that keeps all 40 skills on screen can give 7 mm rows (40 × 7 mm = 280 mm), so every approach makes touch work in two steps: a tap selects, and a card with large Choose / Plan buttons acts. The review bar's "Mark <5 mm targets" outlines the small ones.

Shared by all three

  • Portrait grid instead of arrows: every offered portrait grouped by race, each race header carrying its bonuses, filters for male and female, the portrait number and its voice on every tile, and a party badge on portraits already in use.
  • Class picker with each class's Grandmaster skills on its card and a structured mastery table for this race: rows are skills, columns are the starting class and every promotion (for example Thief, Rogue, Assassin, Spy), with the ranks a promotion adds shaded. The same table opens from "Mastery table" on any member.
  • Skill card (hover or right button on desktop, tap on touch): description, then one row per mastery with its effect and whether this character has it now, after a named promotion, or never; who in the party has or plans the skill.
  • Racial bonuses in plain sight: ▲/▼ attributes with their point cost and maximum, race skill rules grouped by effect ("+1 mastery rank: Merchant, Perception"), MAW's +3 skills and resistances, and race tags on the affected skills.
  • Planner: Plan mode (or the card's "Plan for later") flags skills a member should learn later, including ones that only open after promotion; plans appear as hollow party markers and dashed blue outlines.
  • Voices: "Voice N" is portrait #N's own voice, so a portrait and its voice share a number (the two dragons that share a voice show it). The voice picker lists voices of the same kind with the portraits that use them and plays real lines (Choose me, Hello, Yes, Thanks, Victory… and "all 84"), decoded from the game's IMA ADPCM WAVs.

Open questions

See README.md for the design notes, data sources and the full list. In short:

  • A, B or C, and should C's board overlay stay?
  • Keep the paperdoll? None of the mockups shows it; C could fit a small one beside the portrait.
  • Voice choices: same kind only (as today) or all voices behind a toggle (as here)? And do you want the "Voice N = portrait #N" naming?
  • Should the planner be saved with the party (a journal note or party sheet marker) or exist only during creation?
  • On phones, should menus use the full 20:9 width instead of a pillarboxed 16:9 canvas? That alone gives 25 % more room.
