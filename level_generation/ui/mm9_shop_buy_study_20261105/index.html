MM9 Buy View

Five ways to show an MM9 vendor's goods on the Buy screen. Sell, identify, repair and the party inventory screens stay exactly as they are; only what you see after choosing Buy Standard or Buy Special changes. Every mockup uses the real checked-in stock, item art and native Obsidian frame.

What MM9 did, and what we have

MM9 never put goods on a shelf. Its MERCHANT-COORDS.txt places a scrolling list of 32×32 icons with a 168-pixel name column, filter tabs (Weapons, Armor, Gloves/Boots, Jewelry, Potions/Misc, Quest), a scrollbar, and Buy/Sell buttons with running totals. Repair, Identify and Exit sit in the middle column under the four party faces.

The stock size is in the game code (object.lto), not the table. On each restock a shop is emptied and refilled with 30 random rolls: 15 from each of its two treasure-level ranges. The Quanity and Merchandise columns are never read, so magic shops named for one school sell spells from every school. Rolls are independent, so copies are common.

Our importer did the opposite: it sized every page to the MM6–MM8 shelf it is drawn on. That is where the problems below come from.

What an original MM9 shop held

Per restock (every 2 weeks, or 2 months in Frosgard): 30 rolls. Each roll picks a treasure level in the shop's range, converts it to an item level with a random table, then picks a random item of that level among the types the shop sells. Averages below come from 4,000 simulated restocks per shop using MASTERITEMLIST.csv.

Thjorgard's Ancient Scrolls, for example, put about 30 skill books on its list, but only about 14 different titles, mostly level-1 books. A Guberland "Light Magic" shop showed about 20 different spells from all four schools. Our generated pages hold 6–12 per lane instead.

Recommendation

A, the ledger, for MM9 vendors. It is the original MM9 behaviour translated into our reading surface and fonts, so it fits both worlds at once. It is the only view that shows any number of items without paging or cropping, that groups by category, and that shows prices and stats together, which suits MM9's text-heavy goods: spellbooks, skill books, reagents and rings that look alike as pictures. For Android or controller play, the same rows are already large touch targets.

B is the low-cost fallback. It reuses the inventory/chest grid we already draw and hit-test, and it reads as an extension of the party inventory beside it. It loses on books and spellbooks, which become a wall of identical covers.

Stock data should change with either choice. Once the Buy view no longer has 6, 8 or 12 fixed places, the vendor generator can stop padding pages: drop the 706 duplicate slots, or emit them as a quantity (the runtime table already has a quantity column, always 1 today). The importer's "slot counts match the visual layout" check should become a per-profile maximum. Buy Special could also become a filter in the same list (only enchanted items) rather than a second identical list.
