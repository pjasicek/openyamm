# The Hesper stage

One isolated asset root, `output/hesper_stage/assets_editor_dev` (git-ignored), that mounts the `hesper` world and
owns every Hesper registration. **Nothing Hesper goes into `assets_dev`**: the stage mirrors it (links for what it
does not change, copies for what it does) and adds Hesper on top. The copied `engine/data_tables` follow
`assets_dev`: every run copies in a table that is new there or differs from the stage copy in time or size (a copy keeps
its source's), and rebuilds the tables it appends Hesper rows to from the current `assets_dev` source.

```sh
python3 level_generation/projects/hesper_stage/stage.py          # after any map build; incremental
./tools/run_game.sh --isolated hesper --world hesper --map hes_out1.odm \
    --assets "$PWD/output/hesper_stage/assets_editor_dev"
```

The world's `start` map is `hes_out1.odm` (2026-10-22): new games begin at the crash (hesper_out1 README, The arrival).

Audit every staged BLV/ODM decoration against its project's authored names and the append-only
[`decoration_ids.yml`](decoration_ids.yml) registry:

```sh
python3 level_generation/projects/hesper_stage/check_decoration_ids.py
```

The checker takes the shared stage lock, changes no files, lists each Hesper placement and prints per-map counts.
Exit 1 means mismatched IDs or missing/ambiguous source data. Re-author and compile affected maps with their own
scripts under the builder guide's locks, restage, then rerun the audit.

The ids are the world's decoration numbers 3001-3999 (a map entity stores the number - 1). They were 674-934, rows
appended to the stage's copy of the engine table, until 2026-10-29, when every map was rebuilt with the new ids;
now engine rows added later no longer move them. A map build reads them from `output/hesper_stage/decoration_ids.json`
(written by stage.py from the registry), so restage before building a map that places a new decoration.

The world's icons are a declared icon package (`worlds/hesper x2`). The engine's `icons_x2` is a tree of hard links,
not a directory link: the HUD finds no icons through a linked icons directory.

Headless scenarios run from a directory whose `settings.ini` points at the stage:
`output/hesper_stage/scenario_run/settings.ini` with `[assets] root=<stage>/assets_editor_dev`. Run them as the builder
guide says (`flock -s output/hesper_stage/locks/engine_build.lock <command>`), and stage under
`output/hesper_stage/locks/stage.lock`: a scenario that starts while stage.py rewrites npc.txt sees broken tables. [`talk_topics.yml`](talk_topics.yml) checks `npcs.yml`'s talk (the Pilot's lists at the wreck, after M4
and after the choice, The Deepwell; the Warden's Bounties and the board; Aster's lists, her quest bits and her
silence after Tear it out). The engine drops a topic's Lua error silently: check new topic Lua by its effects or in the
game.

**Monsters** (2026-10-22): 11 rows staged (10065-10066 the Landfall turrets, 10067-10069 the guard's three ranks,
10073-10078 the villagers' six outfits). Their ids lie past the engine's relation table (`hostile.txt`); since 2026-10-23 their relations
are the world's `relations.yml` (above): the defenders fight Kreegans, the town is friendly. The guard and villager models are the reduced
`hes_*_reduce_claude` exports (the guard's with the corpse floor fix and the key-time fix of 2026-10-22).
The turrets' bindings get `stride_length` and `upper_body_root` from `monsters.yml` (the engine requires both of every
binding; their project's draft has neither).
Since 2026-10-23 stage.py also adds `shield_break: {react: fall_back}` to the tactics of every staged monster whose
design shield-break reaction (`monster_hesper.txt`) is "Falls back" (the Trooper, the Ashrunner Gunner).
Tactics (2026-10-22): the turrets traverse to their target and hold their outward facing, the guards placed `immobile`
are posts that still fight within a leash, and every guard fights from its rifle's range (`monsters.yml` `tactics`,
engine: `devdocs/ACTOR_SCRIPTING_INVESTIGATION.md`, Tactics; check: `hesper_out1/landfall_defenders.yml`).

Starport Reach's hostile monsters (2026-10-22, `hesper_out1/spawns.yml`): 10050-10051 the Seeker and Hunter Drones,
10080-10082 the Glassbacks (Pup, Glassback, Bull), 10070-10072 the Ashrunners, 10010 the Trooper (the skinned-shell
caste GLB). The Drones and Glassbacks fill the map's encounter slots (`maps.yml` `slots`, written into the map_stats
row's Mon1-Mon3 columns), so `monsters.yml` gives their rows MM-style pictures (`picture: hes_drone A`): the engine
finds a slot's tiers as "<slot picture> A/B/C". They are hostile through the world's `relations.yml` (2026-10-23; before, the
map's load event set `evt.SetMonsterRelation(id, 0, 4)`).
